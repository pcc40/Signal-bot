Choose a villa renovation company in Dubai on evidence, not on the portfolio. Check that it is licensed for the work and insured, see a villa it finished a few years ago, give it the same written scope as everyone else, and compare priced quotes line by line. Then agree payment terms that keep you in control. A cheap quote on a vague brief is the most expensive thing in renovation.

Demand for renovation in Dubai is high, and so is the number of firms offering it. In 2024 The National reported homeowners paying extra to put right poor work, and recommended detailed written contracts, milestone payments rather than large upfront sums, and checking contractors through past projects ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2024/07/29/high-demand-for-renovations-leaves-some-dubai-homeowners-paying-extra-to-fix-poor-work)). Here is how to do that in practice, from the builder's side.

## Ten checks before you hire

| Check | What to ask for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Licence | The trade licence, with activities that cover the work | An unlicensed or wrongly licensed firm cannot obtain permits and leaves you exposed |
| 2. Permits and community access | Confirmation it can register with your developer for access permits | Without them the crew cannot enter; Nakheel, for example, requires access permits and a security deposit |
| 3. Insurance | Contractor's all-risks and third-party liability certificates | Damage to your house or a neighbour's should not become your claim |
| 4. Completed villas | Addresses of two or three villas finished two or more years ago | Defects show in the first years; new photographs show nothing |
| 5. Owner references | A conversation with those owners | Ask what went wrong and how the firm handled it |
| 6. Who runs your job | The name of the site manager and how often they will be there | A good company with an absent site manager is a bad project |
| 7. Priced scope | A line-by-line quote against your scope | So you can compare and see what is left out |
| 8. Programme | A realistic programme including approvals and long-lead items | Dates that ignore approvals are not dates |
| 9. Payment terms | Stage payments against work done, plus retention | Large deposits remove your leverage |
| 10. Defects and warranty | The defects period, warranties for waterproofing, AC and equipment | What happens after handover is part of the price |

For work that needs a building permit, Dubai Municipality's Dubai BPS app includes a registry of consultants and contractors with ratings ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/new-improved-building-permits-app-launched-for-streamlined-services/)). It is worth checking.

## Get comparable quotes

Most disputes we saw on the contractor's side started with the quote. A one-page price on a vague brief leaves every decision to be argued later, and each one becomes a variation.

1. **Write the scope room by room,** with finishes, or with provisional sums that state an allowance for each item you have not chosen yet.
2. **Send the same scope to every company,** with the same drawings and the same questions.
3. **Ask for a priced bill of quantities or a line-by-line breakdown,** not a single number.
4. **Normalise the quotes.** List what each excludes and price it back in. The lowest headline is often not the lowest real price.
5. **Look at the provisional sums.** A quote full of low allowances will grow.

## Design-and-build company or contractor plus designer?

Many Dubai renovation companies offer design and build: they design the new layout and finishes, then build it. Others build to a design you commission separately.

- **Design-and-build** gives one contract and one point of responsibility, and can be quicker. The trade-off is that the same firm designs, prices and checks its own work, so specification can drift to what suits the firm. Insist on a fixed specification in the contract and an independent check of the work.
- **A separate designer and contractor** gives you independent design and a competitive tender on a complete specification. It needs a finished design before tender and more coordination.

## Red flags

- A price given without seeing the villa, or without a written scope.
- Pressure to pay a large deposit to "secure the slot" or "lock in prices".
- No completed villas you can visit, or references who cannot be reached.
- Reluctance to put changes, prices and dates in writing.
- A programme that does not mention developer approvals or access permits.
- A firm that offers to "sort the approvals later".

## Payment terms that protect you

Agree a payment schedule tied to defined stages you can see on site: strip-out complete, first fix complete, wet areas waterproofed and tested, and so on. Hold a retention until snags are closed after handover. Keep every change in writing with its price and its effect on the programme before it is done. For contract terms, take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer; our guide to [UAE villa construction contracts](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/) explains the clauses that matter most.

## After you choose

The company you choose is only as good as the way the project is run. Agree how site visits will work, ask for a written report with dated photographs, and make sure someone on your side sees the hidden work before it is covered.
