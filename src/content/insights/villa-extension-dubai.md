Extending a villa in Dubai is possible in many communities, but it is never a matter of simply building on. The master developer decides first whether an extension is allowed and on what terms; the authority permit follows; and the real cost sits in tying new structure to an old house. Get the principle agreed before you pay for a full design.

This guide covers the approval route, the rules that limit how much you can add, what owners have reported paying the developer, and how to budget an extension properly. It is written from the builder's side: our founder, Philip Chapman, ran a residential construction company in England for 21 years, and extensions were a large part of that work.

## Who has to approve a villa extension in Dubai?

In a master community there are two approvals, in this order.

**1. The master developer.** Nakheel says owners must obtain a no-objection certificate from its Urban Planning team before making any alterations, and that changes affecting the external façade, structure or footprint need it to confirm the work is permitted at all ([Nakheel](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us)). Emaar Community Management's position is equally direct: "Any alteration to the villa has to be approved by Emaar", applied for through the community portal ([Emaar Community Management](https://www.ecm.ae/en/communities/arabian-ranches/lifestyle/faqs/home-modifications-arabian-ranches/)). Other developers have their own versions.

**2. The authority permit.** An extension adds built-up area and structure, so it needs a building permit from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits. Your registered consultant prepares and submits the drawings. Our guides to [which Dubai authority covers your villa](/insights/which-dubai-authority-covers-my-villa/) and the [Dubai villa building permit](/insights/dubai-municipality-villa-building-permit/) explain that stage.

In Abu Dhabi, permits go through the municipality via TAMM. Minor works, including extensions of up to 25 square metres, pools, canopies and fences, can be permitted directly by the contractor; anything larger follows the full route ([Abu Dhabi DMT](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/en/Media-Centre/News/Department-of-Municipalities-and-Transport-Announces-Amendments-to-Private-Villas-Regulations)). See our [Abu Dhabi villa permit guide](/insights/abu-dhabi-villa-permit-tamm/).

## How much can you add?

There is no single answer, because three sets of rules overlap.

| Rule set | What it controls | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Master developer guidelines | Whether extensions are allowed, footprint, height, appearance, sometimes a charge for added area | Developer or community management, in writing |
| Plot affection plan and planning rules | Setbacks, coverage, number of floors | Your consultant, from the authority |
| Building regulations | Structure, fire, energy, accessibility | Your consultant, at permit stage |

Dubai Municipality's Home First initiative, launched in March 2025, widened the options for Emirati families: second-floor extensions of up to 100%, a minimum villa setback reduced to 1.5 metres, service annexes up to 8 metres over two floors, and a separate villa for a son within the family plot ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/new-home-first-initiative-introduces-flexible-housing-options-for-emirati-families/)). Those rules are aimed at Emirati family housing. In a master community, the developer's guidelines still apply, so do not assume a Home First allowance transfers to your villa without confirmation.

In Abu Dhabi, the September 2025 amendments to the private villa regulations introduced a 1.5 metre front setback and rules for rooftop majlis and annex basements ([Abu Dhabi DMT](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/en/Media-Centre/News/Department-of-Municipalities-and-Transport-Announces-Amendments-to-Private-Villas-Regulations)).

## Does the developer charge for an extension?

Some developers charge for added floor area, and the amount can be significant. In 2018 Gulf News reported Jumeirah Islands owners being charged for extra space, about AED 300 per sq ft by their account, and an owner penalised when unapproved work came to light on sale. Nakheel said applications are assessed case by case and that various costs apply ([Gulf News, 2018](https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/uae/jumeirah-islands-owners-prevented-from-modifying-villas-without-approval-1.1172089)).

Treat that as a reported figure from 2018, not a current tariff. Ask the developer for the current charge in writing, together with the NOC conditions, before you commission full designs. On a 500 sq ft extension, a charge of that order would add around AED 150,000 before a single block is laid, which can change the decision.

## What does a villa extension cost?

We have not found a reliable published rate for villa extensions in Dubai, and we will not invent one. Build the budget up from its parts instead.

| Cost item | How to estimate it |
|---|---|
| New construction | Published rates for new villa space are a starting point: Engel & Völkers (December 2025) gives AED 300–500 per sq ft for standard, 500–600 mid and 900–1,400 luxury ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)). Small extensions usually cost more per sq ft than a whole house, because the fixed costs are spread over less area. |
| Connection to the existing house | Opening up walls, new beams, matching floor levels, waterproofing at the junction. Price it separately; it is rarely in a per sq ft rate. |
| Services | Will the existing air conditioning, electrical supply and drainage cope? Upgrades here can be a large share of the cost. |
| Finishes to match | Matching an existing stone, tile or façade can be expensive or impossible; plan a deliberate change instead. |
| Design and approvals | Consultant fees, developer NOC and any area charge, authority fees. |
| Contingency | Engel & Völkers puts contingency at 10–15% on new builds; on work tied into an older house, in our experience hold more until the existing structure has been opened up. |

## Where extensions go wrong, from the builder's side

- **Foundations that do not match.** A new extension on new foundations settles differently from a house that has stood for twenty years. Without a proper movement joint or a structural design that accounts for it, the crack appears exactly where the old and new meet.
- **Water at the junction.** The most common defect we saw on extensions in England was a leak where the new roof met the old wall. In Dubai, with flat roofs and terraces, the same junction needs a continuous, tested waterproofing detail, not two systems that meet and hope.
- **Services that cannot cope.** An extra bedroom suite and a bigger living room can push an existing air-conditioning system past its capacity. Find out before the design is fixed.
- **Designing before the developer has agreed the principle.** Full drawings for an extension that is refused are wasted fees and months.
- **Treating the extension as separate from the house.** Once the walls are open, owners often decide to redo the kitchen and the bathrooms too. That is a sensible decision, but make it at the start, when it can be priced and programmed properly.

## Extend, renovate or rebuild?

An extension makes sense when the house is sound and the brief is mostly about space. If the brief keeps growing towards new structure, new services, a new envelope and new finishes throughout, compare it honestly with a rebuild: demolition and a new house can be simpler to approve, design and warrant than a heavily extended old one. Our [renovate-or-rebuild tool](/tools/renovate-or-rebuild/) and [guide](/insights/renovate-or-rebuild-villa-dubai/) set out how to compare the two.

## A sensible sequence for an extension

1. Ask the developer whether an extension is allowed on your villa, and what it would charge, in writing.
2. Have the existing house surveyed: structure, roof and terraces, services capacity.
3. Brief your registered consultant with both, and agree a concept the developer will accept in principle.
4. Develop the design far enough to price properly, including the junction with the existing house.
5. Obtain the NOC and the authority permit.
6. Tender to contractors on the same detailed scope and compare like for like.
7. Start on site only when approvals and access permits are in hand.
