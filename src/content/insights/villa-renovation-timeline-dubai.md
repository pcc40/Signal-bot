A villa renovation in Dubai usually takes two to three months on site for a smaller interior refresh and five to seven months for structural work on a large villa, according to published guides. The survey, design, master-developer NOC, any authority permit and choosing the contractor come before that. On our planning allowances, a full renovation of an older villa runs roughly 7 to 13 months from the first survey to handing back the keys, and about half of that passes before anyone starts on site.

This guide sets out each phase, how long it realistically takes, what stretches it in Dubai in particular, and how to keep the months under control. For the whole renovation journey, from first idea to handover, see our [step-by-step plan for a villa renovation](/insights/how-to-plan-a-villa-renovation-dubai/).

## How long does a villa renovation take in Dubai?

Published figures for the work on site vary by scope. [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/villa-renovation-dubai/) lists 60–90 days for an interior, kitchen and bathroom renovation of a three-bedroom townhouse, and 150–210 days for structural work with premium materials on a luxury villa. [A Dubai renovation contractor's 2026 guide](https://smartrenovation.ae/villa-renovation-in-dubai-complete-2026-guide-to-costs-timeline-permits/) puts a smaller, mainly interior project at about two to three months, and says approvals, where needed, may take a few weeks.

| Scope | Published time on site | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller renovation, mainly interior upgrades | About 2–3 months | A Dubai renovation contractor's guide |
| Three-bedroom townhouse: interior, kitchen and bathrooms | 60–90 days | Property Finder |
| Luxury villa: structural work and premium materials | 150–210 days | Property Finder |

Those are the contractor's months. They assume the design is finished, the approvals are in hand and the materials are on order. On an older villa with original services, those three assumptions are where most of the real programme sits.

## What are the phases of a villa renovation, and how long does each take?

The table below is our own rule of thumb for a full renovation of an older villa without an extension: new layout in places, new kitchen and bathrooms, replacement of the main services, new finishes throughout. It is a planning allowance, not a published benchmark. Where a published figure exists, it is noted.

| Phase | Our planning allowance | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Brief, condition survey and opening-up | 2–4 weeks | Access to the villa, specialists' availability, how much is opened up |
| Concept design and cost plan to NOC level | 4–8 weeks | How settled the brief is, structural questions |
| Master-developer NOC | 3–6 weeks, including one round of comments | Nakheel and others assess case by case and publish no single turnaround |
| Detailed design and authority permit, where needed | 2–6 weeks, overlapping the tender | Scope; a renovation contractor's guide says "a few weeks" |
| Tender, comparison and contract | 3–6 weeks | Number of bidders, clarity of the drawings |
| Mobilisation and community site requirements | 1–2 weeks | Deposit, Site Access Permit, fence, workforce permits (Nakheel recommends requesting long-term workforce access permits 3–4 working days before they are needed) |
| Long-lead procurement (kitchens, stone, glazing, plant) | 2–4 months from order, running alongside | Origin, bespoke or standard, shipping and customs |
| Strip-out and opening-up | 2–4 weeks | Size of villa, what the strip-out reveals |
| Structure, waterproofing and MEP first fix | 6–10 weeks | Extent of rewiring, re-plumbing, air conditioning |
| Finishes and second fix | 6–12 weeks | Stone, joinery, owner decisions |
| Testing, commissioning, snagging and handover | 3–6 weeks | Systems complexity, quality of the earlier work |

Added end to end, the phases before the work on site come to roughly 15–32 weeks, and the work on site to roughly 17–32 weeks. Because some phases overlap, our working range for survey to keys is about 7 to 13 months. That is our arithmetic, and it sits comfortably with the published on-site figures above: a well-run renovation of this kind is about five to seven months on site, with three to six months of preparation in front of it.

## Where do the months go before anyone starts on site?

**Survey and opening-up.** Older villas hide their problems behind finishes: tired air-conditioning, original wiring, failing waterproofing in wet areas and on roofs, drainage that has settled. Two to four weeks spent finding out is the cheapest time on the whole programme. Skip it and the same questions are answered after the strip-out, at the contractor's rates and with the crew waiting. Our guide to [villa renovation mistakes](/insights/villa-renovation-mistakes-dubai/) explains why this one matters most.

**Design to NOC level.** The master developer needs to see what will change: layout, structure, façade, pool, external plant. The design should be priced at the same time, so you do not seek approval for something you cannot afford.

**The master-developer NOC.** In Nakheel communities, Nakheel says an NOC from its Urban Planning team is needed before any alteration, requested through its online services ([Nakheel](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us)). It has said that villa modification applications are assessed case by case ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/uae/jumeirah-islands-owners-prevented-from-modifying-villas-without-approval-1.1172089)), and it does not publish a single turnaround. Nakheel approvals carry an expiry date, so check yours and keep the contractor's start within it. Emaar communities follow a similar pattern: in Arabian Ranches, any alteration needs Emaar's approval through a home modification NOC ([Emaar Community Management](https://www.ecm.ae/en/communities/arabian-ranches/lifestyle/faqs/home-modifications-arabian-ranches/)). Our [Nakheel NOC guide](/insights/nakheel-noc-villa-renovation/) covers the process in detail.

**The authority permit.** Where the scope needs one, your DM-registered consultant applies to Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, with service-authority NOCs gathered through Dubai's unified window ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-launches-single-window-for-building-permits/); [Build in Dubai](https://buildindubai.gov.ae/)). Light interior work often needs less, but confirm it in writing.

**Tender and contract.** Pricing a renovation takes contractors longer than pricing a new build, because they are pricing risk as well as work. Give them the survey findings and the approved drawings, and you get prices you can compare. Our guide on [how to choose a villa contractor in Dubai](/insights/how-to-choose-a-villa-contractor-dubai/) covers the comparison.

## What stretches a villa renovation programme in Dubai?

### The summer midday break

From 15 June to 15 September, outdoor work under direct sunlight is banned from 12:30pm to 3pm, under the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation's midday break; 2026 was its 22nd year ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/midday-outdoor-work-ban-returns-across-uae-for-22nd-year-1.500567381); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-midday-break-starts-june-15-work-under-direct-sunlight-banned-during-peak-afternoon-hours?amp=1)). Work that technically cannot be interrupted is exempt. On a renovation the effect falls on roofs, façades, pools and landscaping. A sensible programme does the external work in the cooler months and moves internal trades into the summer. Summer is also when many owners travel, and in our experience decisions slow down with them.

### Ramadan working hours

In February 2026 the Ministry announced that normal private-sector working hours would be reduced by two hours a day during Ramadan ([MoHRE](https://mohre.gov.ae/en/media-center/news/12/2/2026/reduction-of-working-hours-for-private-sector-employees-by-two-hours-daily-during-the-holy-month-of)). Ramadan follows the lunar calendar, so it moves earlier each year. Contractors adjust shifts, but a month of shorter days is a month of lower output, and it should appear in the programme rather than as a surprise.

### NOC and permit comments

Every round of comments costs weeks. The usual causes are drawings that do not show clearly what is changing, and design changes after submission. Changing what was approved, such as enclosing a terrace that was approved as open, may need to go back to the master developer before work can continue.

### Long-lead items

Bespoke joinery, imported stone, large glazing, specialist ironmongery and air-conditioning plant are commonly the items that decide the finish date. Our allowance of two to four months from order to site is a rule of thumb; get real lead times from suppliers at design stage. Our guide to [sourcing materials abroad](/insights/sourcing-materials-abroad-dubai-villa/) covers shipping and customs timing.

### What the strip-out finds

No survey sees everything. Corroded pipework in a slab, wiring that cannot be reused or a beam that is not where the drawings show it will all add time. A realistic programme carries some float after the strip-out, just as the budget carries contingency; Engel & Völkers says 10–15% of total project cost is widely recommended for contingency ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)).

## What the contractor's programme does not tell you

On the contractor's side, the programme we submitted at tender always assumed that the client's decisions would arrive on time. The first float to disappear on most jobs was the time allowed for the owner to choose tiles, stone, sanitaryware and joinery, and once it was gone every late choice became a delay the client had caused. Our founder, Philip Chapman, ran P Chapman Construction in the UK for 21 years, and the lesson travels: the dates that matter most on a renovation programme are the owner's decision dates, not the builder's activity bars.

So ask for a programme that shows, alongside the construction activities:

- the date each finish has to be chosen, and the date it has to be ordered;
- the NOC, permit and site-access milestones, with the start of works tied to them;
- the summer and Ramadan periods, and which work is planned for each;
- inspections and tests that have to pass before the next trade can follow.

## How can you shorten a villa renovation timeline?

1. **Survey early.** Open up the villa before the design is fixed, ideally before you buy if the renovation is the reason for buying.
2. **Freeze the scope and finishes before tender.** Every change after the start is a variation, priced and programmed by the contractor.
3. **Overlap sensibly.** Detailed design can run alongside the permit, and the tender alongside both, without much risk.
4. **Order long-lead items at design freeze.** Not after the strip-out.
5. **Tie the start date to approvals in hand.** A commencement date fixed at signature, with the NOC still pending, invites a delay claim.
