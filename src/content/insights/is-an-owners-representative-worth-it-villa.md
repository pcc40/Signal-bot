An owner's representative is worth it on a villa when three things are true: a lot of money is at stake, the owner cannot give the project the time or the construction knowledge it needs, and there is no one else on the team whose only interest is the owner's. When all three apply, an independent client-side project manager usually pays for itself in avoided cost and avoided stress. When none apply, you may not need one, and we would rather say so.

This is the longer version of the answer on our [fees page](/fees/). It is written by a firm that sells the service, so we set out the cases against it as well as the cases for it.

## What are you actually paying for?

On a villa in Dubai, almost everyone you hire has a commercial interest of their own. The contractor is paid to build and earns more on variations. The designer is paid to design and is judged on how the house looks. The registered consultant has statutory duties to the authority. Suppliers want to sell. Each can be excellent and still not be on your side when their interest and yours part company.

A client-side project manager, or owner's representative, is the one adviser paid only by you, with no commission from anyone else, whose job is to test what the others tell you before you commit. In practice that means:

- **A brief and a budget that agree with each other** before design goes too far.
- **Bids you can compare** line by line, with exclusions priced back in.
- **A contract and payment schedule** that tie money to work you have seen, reviewed alongside your lawyer.
- **Variations challenged** before they are agreed, not after they are invoiced.
- **Independent site visits** at the moments that matter, with written reports and dated photographs.
- **Decisions made on time**, so the programme does not stall waiting for you.

None of this replaces the registered consultant. In Dubai, only consultancies registered with Dubai Municipality may practise engineering consultancy, including supervision ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/dubai-issues-new-law-regulating-engineering-consultancy-activities-key-takeaway-points/)). We work alongside your consultant and contractor; we do not take on their statutory roles. Our article on [owner's representative, project manager and consultant](/insights/owners-representative-vs-project-manager-vs-consultant/) sets out who does what.

## When is an owner's representative worth it?

| Your situation | Is it worth it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You live abroad or travel most of the month | Usually yes | Someone has to see the work before it is covered and report honestly; photos sent by the contractor are not the same thing |
| Large new build or full renovation | Usually yes | More money, more packages and more interfaces between designer, contractor and specialists |
| Older villa with unknown services and alterations | Usually yes | Unknowns turn into variations; someone needs to price and challenge them as they arise |
| You have never built or renovated before | Often yes | The first project is where the expensive lessons are learned |
| A stalled project or a contractor dispute | Often yes | A neutral view of what was built, what was paid and what is left is the starting point for any rescue |
| A small refresh with a contractor you know, and you live here | Probably not | A clear scope, a fixed price and staged payments may be enough |
| You are a builder or engineer with time to give it | Probably not | You can do much of the role yourself |

The common thread is exposure. The more money you commit before you can see the result, and the less able you are to check it yourself, the more an independent check is worth.

## Where does the value come from?

We will not quote you a percentage saving. No honest firm can promise one, because the outcome depends on the design, the contractor, the market and your own decisions. What we can describe is where villa budgets go wrong, and which of those losses a client-side project manager is there to prevent.

### An incomplete scope

On the contractor's side, the first thing we priced on any job was what the drawings did not say. A vague scope is not a cheaper project; it is a project whose price is decided later, one variation at a time, when you have no alternative contractor. A client-side project manager makes sure the scope is complete enough to price before you sign.

### Over-design and over-specification

Over-design is a real risk here. In August 2025 Dubai Municipality warned villa engineering consultancies against over-designing structures and inflating build costs ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-municipality-warns-consultancies-inflating-villa-designs-raising-costs-1.500245704)). An owner-side reviewer asks the questions an owner might not think to ask: is this specification needed, what is the cheaper alternative, and what does it do to the programme?

### Payments ahead of progress

A large deposit or a payment certified before the work is done removes your leverage. Checking that each payment matches what is actually on site is one of the simplest and most valuable things anyone on your side can do.

### Defects found too late

Waterproofing, insulation, pipework and cabling disappear behind finishes. If no one on your side sees them before they are covered, a defect found later costs far more to put right. The National reported in 2024 that some Dubai homeowners were paying extra to fix poor renovation work, and recommended detailed written contracts, milestone payments and checks on contractors' past projects ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2024/07/29/high-demand-for-renovations-leaves-some-dubai-homeowners-paying-extra-to-fix-poor-work)). Our guide to [owner site visits](/insights/owner-site-visits-villa-build/) lists the moments that matter.

### Slow decisions

On site, waiting is expensive. A crew stood down while the owner chooses a tile, or a programme held while a variation is argued over email, costs money even when no one invoices it. Someone on your side whose job is to put decisions in front of you early, with the options priced, keeps the work moving.

### The contingency you do not spend

Industry guidance recommends holding a contingency of 10–15% of total project cost ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)). Whether that contingency survives to the end of the project is, in our experience, the clearest single test of how well a villa was run.

## When is it not worth it?

We would rather you hired no one than hired the wrong kind of firm. An owner's representative is not good value when:

- **The job is small and well defined.** A bathroom or a kitchen with a contractor you trust, a fixed price and staged payments rarely justifies a separate fee.
- **The firm is not independent.** If your "representative" takes commissions from contractors or suppliers, or is part of the design-and-build company, you are paying for a voice that is not only yours. Ask in writing.
- **Nobody will actually come to site.** A monthly report written from the contractor's photographs is not independent reporting. Ask who will visit, by name, and how often at each stage.
- **The fee is not tied to a scope.** A percentage with no written list of services and visits tells you little about what you are buying.

Our guide to [choosing a client-side project manager](/insights/how-to-choose-client-side-project-manager/) has the questions to ask before you appoint one.

## How should you judge the fee?

There is no published Dubai benchmark for client-side project management fees, so be wary of anyone who quotes a "standard" percentage. Compare fees on what you get:

1. **The written scope.** Stages covered, reports, meetings and the number of site visits at each stage.
2. **Who does the work.** The named person who will attend site and the person who stands behind them.
3. **The basis.** Percentage, monthly or fixed per stage, and what happens to the fee if the build overruns.
4. **Independence.** A written statement that the firm takes nothing from anyone else on your project.

## A builder's view

Our founder, Philip Chapman, ran a premium residential construction company in England for 21 years. From the contractor's side, the clients whose projects went best were rarely the ones who pushed hardest on price. They were the ones whose questions came early, whose decisions arrived on time and whose payments matched the work. Sometimes that was the owner themselves. On larger houses, it was usually because someone on the owner's side was doing that job properly.
