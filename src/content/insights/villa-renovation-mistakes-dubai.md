The villa renovations that go wrong in Dubai rarely go wrong on site. They go wrong in the months before: a scope that is still moving when the contractor starts, an existing villa that nobody opened up before it was priced, finishes chosen after the strip-out, and approvals that run behind the programme. Each of these is avoidable, and each one is cheaper to fix on paper than in the building.

We spent 21 years on the contractor's side of premium residential work in the UK. The mistakes below are the ones we saw owners make again and again, described the way the builder sees them. None of them is about choosing a bad contractor. A good contractor working to a moving brief will still deliver a late, over-budget job, and will be entitled to be paid for it.

## Why do villa renovations run over budget?

Renovation budgets leak through the gaps between what was priced and what is built. On a new build the drawings describe almost everything. On a renovation the existing villa hides things, and every surprise becomes a price agreed after the contract is signed, when the owner has the least bargaining power. Our [step-by-step plan for a villa renovation in Dubai](/insights/how-to-plan-a-villa-renovation-dubai/) shows the order that avoids most of the mistakes below.

Published figures show how wide the range already is before anything goes wrong. [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/villa-renovation-dubai/) and [a Dubai renovation contractor's 2026 guide](https://smartrenovation.ae/villa-renovation-in-dubai-complete-2026-guide-to-costs-timeline-permits/) give different bands for the same kinds of work, which is one reason we never let an owner budget from a single headline rate. Our [villa renovation cost guide](/insights/villa-renovation-cost-dubai/) sets the sources side by side.

## Mistake 1: starting on site before the scope is frozen

This is the most expensive habit we saw, and the most common. The owner is keen to start, the contractor is happy to mobilise, and the design is "nearly there". The kitchen layout will be settled next week. The bathroom tiles are being chosen. The pergola might become a covered terrace.

From the builder's side, every one of those open questions is an opportunity. Once the contract is signed and the site is running, a change is a [variation](/glossary/#variation), priced at the contractor's rates, with an adjustment to the programme that the contractor writes first. Nobody tenders against a variation. On a renovation with a moving brief, the variation account can grow steadily without anyone noticing the total until the final account.

The fix is simple to state and hard to hold to: freeze the layout, the specification and the main finishes before the job is tendered, and treat any change after that as a decision with a price and a date attached. Our article on [what a builder knows that most owners don't](/insights/what-the-contractor-knows/) explains how contractors price this risk.

## Mistake 2: not opening up the existing villa before it is priced

Older villas in established communities such as [Jumeirah Islands](/areas/jumeirah-islands/), [Arabian Ranches](/areas/arabian-ranches/) and the [Palm Jumeirah](/areas/palm-jumeirah/) were built to the standards and services of their day. What sits behind the plaster, under the screed and above the ceilings is often original: wiring, plumbing, drainage, waterproofing and air-conditioning ductwork.

If nobody has looked, the contractor cannot price it. So the unknowns go into the quote as [provisional sums](/glossary/#provisional-sum) or exclusions. On the contractor's side, a provisional sum was a placeholder we expected to grow, and it usually did once the walls were opened and the real condition of the services was clear.

A condition survey, with some opening-up of walls, ceilings and wet areas, costs a small fraction of the works and turns guesses into a scope. It is also the evidence you need if the honest answer turns out to be a rebuild rather than a renovation: see [renovate or rebuild](/insights/renovate-or-rebuild-villa-dubai/).

## Mistake 3: choosing finishes late

Owners often leave the enjoyable decisions until the end: stone, tiles, joinery, sanitaryware, lighting. The trouble is that many of these items have long lead times, and the programme cannot finish until they arrive.

In our experience the finish date of a renovation is usually set by the slowest item to arrive, not by the site work. Bespoke kitchens and wardrobes, imported stone, large-format glazing and replacement air-conditioning plant are typical examples. If they are chosen after the strip-out, the site waits, and a contractor who is waiting is usually entitled to more time and sometimes more money.

Choose the long-lead items during design, confirm them with signed samples, and order them against a programme date. If you are buying items yourself, read our guide to [sourcing materials abroad](/insights/sourcing-materials-abroad-dubai-villa/) first: owner-supplied items move risk onto you.

## Mistake 4: letting the programme run ahead of the approvals

Most villa renovations in master-planned communities need the master developer's approval before the authority permit. In Nakheel communities the process starts with a [Nakheel NOC](/insights/nakheel-noc-villa-renovation/) application through its portal, and Nakheel's own [customer FAQs](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us) cover NOCs, access permits and contractor requirements. Owners in at least one Nakheel community have been [stopped from modifying villas without approval](https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/uae/jumeirah-islands-owners-prevented-from-modifying-villas-without-approval-1.1172089).

The building permit then comes from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, through the [single window](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-launches-single-window-for-building-permits/) and the [Build in Dubai](https://buildindubai.gov.ae/) platform. Your DM-registered consultant makes the submissions, and the design must meet the current [Dubai Building Code](https://www.dm.gov.ae/municipality-business/planning-and-construction/dubai-building-code-2/).

The mistake is to book the contractor's start date first and hope the approvals catch up. A contractor mobilised with nothing they are allowed to do still costs money, in site set-up and in [preliminaries](/glossary/#preliminaries), which Turner & Townsend puts at around 12% of cost on a small Dubai project in its [2025 market intelligence](https://marketintelligence.turnerandtownsend.com/uaemi-2025/construction-cost-performance/). Plan the start around the approvals, not the other way round.

## Mistake 5: comparing quotes on the bottom line

Three quotes for the same renovation can differ widely, and the cheapest is rarely the cheapest outcome. The differences usually sit in what each contractor has left out: different allowances for kitchens and finishes, provisional sums set at different levels, preliminaries priced differently, and exclusions buried at the back.

Before comparing totals, put every quote onto the same basis: the same scope, the same allowances, the same treatment of provisional sums and the same exclusions. Our guide on [how to choose a villa contractor](/insights/how-to-choose-a-villa-contractor-dubai/) covers the checks, and a [contractor quote review](/services/consultant-and-contractor-selection/#quote-review) does it for you before you sign.

## Mistake 6: paying ahead of the work

Large advances and payment schedules that run ahead of progress are common, and they hand the leverage to the contractor. If the job slows, or a dispute starts, the owner has already paid for work that has not been done.

Pay against measured work and agreed milestones, and hold [retention](/glossary/#retention) until the defects are closed out. Dubai Municipality publishes [guidance contract forms for private villas](https://www.dm.gov.ae/municipality-business/guidance-contracts-forms-for-engineering-consulting-services-and-construction-contracting-for-private-villas/) that are a sensible starting point; take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer on the contract you sign.

## Mistake 7: agreeing changes on site, by word of mouth

Some changes are unavoidable: something is found behind a wall, or a decision looks different once the room exists. The mistake is agreeing them in a conversation on site and finding out the price at the final account.

Every change should go through a written change request with a price and a time effect, approved in writing before the work is done, and recorded in a decision log. It is dull, and it is the single best protection an owner has against a final account that nobody recognises.

## Should you live in the villa while it is renovated?

For a light refresh, sometimes. For a full renovation with new services, waterproofing or structural work, it usually costs more than it saves. The contractor has to phase the work around the family, keep power, water and cooling running, and protect occupied rooms, all of which takes time and money. In summer, the [midday break](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-midday-break-starts-june-15-work-under-direct-sunlight-banned-during-peak-afternoon-hours?amp=1) from 15 June to 15 September also shortens productive outdoor hours.

## How much contingency should you hold?

[Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai) says a contingency of 10–15% of total project cost is widely recommended. On an older villa with original services, we would plan at the upper end. The important part is who controls it: the [contingency](/glossary/#contingency) is the owner's money, released only against approved changes, not an extra line in the contractor's price.
