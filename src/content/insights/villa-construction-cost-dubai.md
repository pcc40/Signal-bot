Building a villa in Dubai in 2026 costs roughly AED 300–500 per sq ft for standard specification, AED 850–1,200 for luxury and AED 1,200 or more for ultra-luxury, for construction alone. Once you add design fees, authority charges, contingency, the pool, landscaping and furniture, the all-in figure is materially higher. On a 10,000 sq ft luxury villa, our worked example below lands at roughly AED 10.4m to AED 17.7m before loose furniture.

The rate per square foot is the number everyone asks about. It is also the number that matters least once the job is under way. What follows is the full picture, and where the money actually goes.

## How much does it cost per square foot to build a villa in Dubai?

Published sources broadly agree on the shape of the market, if not the exact edges. All of the figures below are construction only.

| Tier | Engel & Völkers (Dec 2025) | Optimal Engineering (Mar 2026) | Knight Frank Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | AED 300–500 / sq ft | AED 350–500 / sq ft | ~AED 390 / sq ft (AED 4,200 / m²) |
| Mid / premium | AED 500–600 / sq ft | AED 600–850 / sq ft | — |
| Luxury | AED 900–1,400 / sq ft | AED 850–1,200 / sq ft | ~AED 1,020 / sq ft (AED 11,000 / m²) high-end |
| Ultra-luxury | — | AED 1,200+ / sq ft | — |

Turner & Townsend's UAE-wide figures for individual detached villas sit in the same bands: about AED 460–560 per sq ft for medium standard and AED 1,000–1,230 for prestige (AED 4,950–6,050 and 10,800–13,200 per m², Q1 2024). Our [cost calculator](/tools/villa-build-cost-calculator/) uses AED 300–500 for standard, 500–850 for premium, 850–1,200 for luxury and 1,200–1,400 or more for ultra-luxury. Currie & Brown, cited by PRASOON, forecast UAE construction cost inflation of around 3% for 2026, which matters on a build that will be priced now and paid for over the next year or two.

Two cautions. First, these are averages across very different projects. A rectangular two-storey villa on a flat plot and a cantilevered, glass-heavy house with a basement are not the same building at the same rate. Second, the tier you are in is decided by the design, not by the brochure. Basements, large spans, structural glazing, stone cladding and imported joinery move a villa up a tier faster than any finishes schedule.

## What is included in the build cost, and what is not?

The contractor's rate normally covers the building itself: substructure, frame, envelope, internal fit-out to the specified standard, and the mechanical, electrical and plumbing installations. It also includes the contractor's [preliminaries](/glossary/#preliminaries), the cost of running the site. Turner & Townsend's 2025 UAE market intelligence puts contractor preliminaries in Dubai at 12% on a 2,500 m² project and 14% on a 50,000 m² one, so a meaningful slice of every square foot is site management, welfare, temporary works, insurance and plant rather than the building you will live in.

What is usually outside the rate:

- Design and engineering fees for the architect and engineers
- Authority, master-developer and utility charges
- Contingency
- Swimming pool and external works, unless specifically included
- Landscaping
- Furniture, fixtures and equipment (FF&E), and often specialist items such as home automation, lighting control, audio-visual and wine rooms
- The owner's own advisers, including any owner's representative

Always read the contractor's list of exclusions before comparing totals. Two bids with the same headline can cover very different buildings.

### Consultant and design fees

Optimal Engineering puts full design and engineering at 6–12% of the construction contract value, with fixed-fee packages of AED 35k–150k for simpler projects. At the other end, Engel & Völkers quotes AED 50k–150k for design, engineering and permits, which reflects a much simpler scope, typically a standard villa on a standard plot. A bespoke luxury villa sits firmly at the percentage end.

Your consultant must be registered with Dubai Municipality. Under Dubai Law No. 14 of 2025, only registered consultancies may practise as engineering consultants, and the consultant's fee normally covers the statutory site role during construction as well as design. Check exactly which stages are in the fee before you sign.

### Authority, NOC and connection fees

A [property information website](https://realestateclubdubai.com/dubai-villa-architects) puts permits, NOCs and DEWA connection at AED 25k–75k for a straightforward villa. Optimal Engineering itemises more: a Dubai Municipality building permit at AED 15k–60k for a standard villa, a permanent DEWA connection at AED 10k–40k, and AED 80k–250k for authority approvals in total. The range is wide because it depends on the plot, the master developer's own charges, and the size of the electrical load. Your route runs through Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, and usually through the master developer first.

### Contingency

Engel & Völkers and Optimal Engineering both put it at 10–15%. We would add one qualification from experience: contingency is not a fixed percentage, it is a measure of how much of the building is still undecided when you sign. A fully resolved design with a complete finishes schedule can carry less. A design with half the specification still described as "to be confirmed" needs more.

### Pools, landscaping and FF&E

Optimal Engineering prices a pool at AED 80k–120k for a basic fibreglass pool and AED 150k–250k for a standard concrete one, and AED 400k or more for a luxury pool. For landscaping and furniture we have found no industry source; a [property information website](https://realestateclubdubai.com/dubai-villa-architects) gives landscaping at AED 100–500 per sq ft of garden and FF&E at 30–40% of build cost at the top end of the market. FF&E is the line owners most often forget, and on a luxury villa it can be one of the largest. If stone, joinery or fittings are coming from abroad, add shipping, duty and lead time to the budget early; see [sourcing materials abroad for a Dubai villa](/insights/sourcing-materials-abroad-dubai-villa/).

## What does a 10,000 sq ft luxury villa cost to build in Dubai?

Here is a worked example using the published ranges above. It is illustrative, not a quotation. We assume 10,000 sq ft of built-up area, luxury specification at AED 850–1,200 per sq ft (Optimal Engineering, the same band as our calculator), and 4,000 sq ft of landscaped garden. For authority charges we use the top of the fixed range as a floor and 2% of build as a prudent ceiling; that is our own rule of thumb for a villa this size.

| Line | Low | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction | AED 8,500,000 | AED 12,000,000 | 10,000 sq ft × AED 850 / 1,200 |
| Design and engineering | AED 510,000 | AED 1,440,000 | 6% of 8.5m / 12% of 12.0m |
| Authority, NOCs, connections | AED 75,000 | AED 240,000 | Fixed floor / 2% of 12.0m |
| Contingency | AED 850,000 | AED 1,800,000 | 10% of 8.5m / 15% of 12.0m |
| Pool | AED 80,000 | AED 250,000 | Fibreglass / standard concrete |
| Landscaping | AED 400,000 | AED 2,000,000 | 4,000 sq ft × AED 100 / 500 |
| **Subtotal before FF&E** | **AED 10,415,000** | **AED 17,730,000** | |
| FF&E | AED 2,550,000 | AED 4,800,000 | 30% of 8.5m / 40% of 12.0m |
| **Total including FF&E** | **AED 12,965,000** | **AED 22,530,000** | |

The arithmetic is simple; the point is the spread. Construction is roughly 68–82% of the total before FF&E, and less again once furniture is added. If you only budget the per-square-foot rate, you are budgeting perhaps two thirds of the project.

The range is also wide because the low column pairs the cheapest assumptions together and the high column the most expensive. A real project sits somewhere inside it, and the brief decides where. Inflation of around 3% for 2026 should be layered on top if contracts will be signed late in the year.

## Where do villa budgets leak?

Our founder spent 21 years running a premium building company whose houses won RIBA and LABC recognition. From that seat, budgets almost never failed on the rate. They failed on the gaps around it.

**Provisional sums.** When a design is incomplete, the contractor carries an allowance for the unknown item: a kitchen, a staircase, a lighting system. The allowance is often set low so the overall bid looks competitive. When the real item is chosen, the difference becomes an addition. On the contractor's side, a bid with a lot of provisional sums was always a bid with a lot of room in it, for us.

**Late decisions.** A change on paper costs a drawing revision. The same change after the slab is poured costs demolition, re-work, a programme delay and the contractor's time-related site costs for every extra week. Finishes, sanitaryware, glazing systems and automation chosen late are the most common culprits.

**Variations priced without competition.** Once a contractor is appointed, every change is priced by one firm, with no competing bid to keep it honest. Variation rates are often higher than the rates in the original price.

**Over-design.** In August 2025, Dubai Municipality warned villa consultancies about over-designing structures and inflating build costs (Gulf News). An over-specified frame or foundation is cost you pay for without seeing it.

**Delay.** Every extra month on site means another month of the contractor's preliminaries. With preliminaries at 12–14% of the contract (Turner & Townsend), a long overrun is expensive before a single extra brick is laid. ## How do you protect a villa construction budget?

- **Finish the design before you price it.** The more of the building that is drawn and specified at bid stage, the fewer provisional sums and the more real competition you get.
- **Set the budget from the brief, not the rate.** Use the full list above, including fees, external works and FF&E.
- **Compare bids like-for-like.** Normalise exclusions, provisional sums and preliminaries before you look at totals. Our guide to [choosing a villa contractor in Dubai](/insights/how-to-choose-a-villa-contractor-dubai/) explains how.
- **Hold the contingency yourself.** It should sit in the owner's budget, released by decision, not buried in the contractor's price.
- **Price every change before it is instructed.** Agree cost and time effect in writing first.
- **Track cost monthly against the programme.** Payments should follow work actually done, not dates on a calendar.
- **Read the contract before signing it.** Our guide to [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/) covers the clauses that matter, and you should take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer.
