In August 2025 Dubai Municipality warned several engineering consultancy offices for inflating the structural designs of villas. It said the designs went beyond what the Dubai Building Code requires, had no proven engineering need, and pushed up construction costs for the owners. For anyone building a villa in Dubai, it is a rare official statement of a risk that usually goes unseen: paying for structure the house does not need.

## What did Dubai Municipality say?

The announcement was made on 26 August 2025. According to the Municipality's own release ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/warnings-issued-to-consultancy-offices-for-inflating-villa-designs/)):

- **Who was warned.** Several engineering consultancy offices in Dubai.
- **What was found.** Exaggerated structural designs for citizens' villas that contravened Dubai Building Code requirements.
- **Why it matters.** The designs caused unjustified increases in construction cost with no proven engineering need.
- **What came before.** Earlier circulars to all consultancy offices in the emirate had already required strict compliance with the code and approved engineering standards.
- **What happens next.** The Municipality said it will monitor consultancy offices and contractors to limit excessive use of construction materials, including steel.

Eng. Maryam Al Muhairi, CEO of the Municipality's Buildings Regulation and Permits Agency, described compliance with the code as "a professional and ethical responsibility", with the aim of reducing cost "without compromising safety and quality" ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/warnings-issued-to-consultancy-offices-for-inflating-villa-designs/)).

## What are the consequences for consultants?

The Municipality said repeated violations would negatively affect an office's annual evaluation and could lead to disciplinary measures. It also noted that earlier in 2025 it had suspended two engineering consultancy offices and barred them from licensing new projects for six months, for practices that breached regulations and professional ethics ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-municipality-warns-consultancies-inflating-villa-designs-raising-costs-1.500245704)).

## What is over-design, and why does it happen?

Over-design is structure sized well beyond what the code and the loads call for: more reinforcing steel, thicker slabs, larger columns or heavier foundations than the engineering justifies. The Municipality did not publish quantities or thresholds, and we will not guess at them.

From the contractor's side, the reasons are usually ordinary rather than sinister:

- **Caution is free for the designer.** A heavier structure carries less risk for the engineer, and the owner pays for it.
- **Speed.** Reusing a conservative design from a previous villa is quicker than optimising a new one.
- **Fees linked to cost.** Where a fee is a percentage of construction cost, there is no reward for making the building cheaper.
- **Nobody asks.** Most owners never see a structural drawing, and the contractor is paid to build what is drawn.

The Dubai Building Code already contains the safety margins the authority considers necessary. Building beyond it does not buy meaningful extra safety. It buys concrete and steel.

## What does it mean for your villa?

The warning concerned citizens' villas, but the same code applies to every villa in Dubai. Structure is one of the largest packages in any build. Engel & Völkers puts Dubai villa construction at about AED 300–500 per square foot for a standard build and AED 900–1,400 for luxury ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)), and the frame and foundations are a major part of that. A design that carries more material than it needs raises the tender price, and no contractor will tell you so.

It also affects the programme. More steel to fix and more concrete to pour is more time on site.

## How do you check your own design?

You cannot assess a structure yourself, and nor can we: that is the work of a registered structural engineer. What an owner can do is ask the right questions at the right time.

| When | What to ask | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Before appointing the consultant | How is your fee calculated, and what is your approach to structural efficiency? | A fee tied to cost rewards the wrong thing |
| At concept design | What structural system are you proposing, and what were the alternatives? | The big savings are made early |
| Before tender | Can you take me through the design against the Dubai Building Code? | A good engineer can explain the margins plainly |
| At tender | What are the steel and concrete quantities, and how do they compare between bidders? | Unusual quantities show up when bids are side by side |
| On a large or complex villa | Should a second registered engineer review the structural design? | An independent review costs little against the structure it checks |

None of this is about cutting corners. The Municipality's own stated aim is "to strike a balance between construction quality and reduced costs".

## A caution on the other side

The answer to over-design is not under-design. Do not accept a contractor's offer to "save steel" on site, and do not let anyone change a structural element without the registered consultant's written approval and, where needed, the authority's. Savings belong in the design, made by the engineer responsible for it, before the permit is issued.
