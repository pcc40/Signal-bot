Most owners start by deciding what they want the villa to look like. The better first question is what the house needs. A refresh changes surfaces, a refit replaces rooms and fittings, and a full renovation renews the services behind the walls and often the layout. The condition of the building usually decides which one you are really doing, and finding that out before you choose is the cheapest step in the whole project.

## What is the difference?

| Level | What it covers | Approvals to expect | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refresh | Paint, flooring, lighting, soft furnishings, some joinery | Least; community notice and contractor entry permits | Low, if the house underneath is sound |
| Refit | New kitchens, bathrooms, wardrobes and finishes, layouts largely unchanged | Developer approval for most refits; authority permit if services or structure change | Medium: wet areas expose old pipes, drains and waterproofing |
| Full renovation | Renewed air-conditioning, electrics, plumbing, drainage and waterproofing, often layout changes | Developer approval and, usually, an authority permit through a registered consultant | Higher: the scope depends on what is found when the house is opened |

On approvals, Dubai Municipality said in 2023 that interior and exterior painting no longer needs a maintenance permit ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fdubai-launches-new-self-permit-application-service-for-minor-building-structural-repairs-1.1691992197616)). Master developers set their own rules: Emaar's Arabian Ranches guidance, for example, says interior painting and regular maintenance can go ahead without an NOC, while any alteration to the villa needs Emaar's approval ([Emaar Community Management](https://www.ecm.ae/en/communities/arabian-ranches/lifestyle/faqs/home-modifications-arabian-ranches/)). Our guide to [changing a villa's layout](/insights/changing-villa-layout-dubai-approvals/) covers when internal changes need a permit.

## What should decide it?

The house, more than the brief. Four questions settle most cases:

1. **Are the services near the end of their life?** Air-conditioning, wiring and boards, water pipes and drainage. If they are, a refit puts new rooms in front of systems that will fail behind them. Our guide to [rewiring and replumbing an older Dubai villa](/insights/older-villa-mep-renovation/) explains what to check.
2. **Is the envelope sound?** Roof and terrace waterproofing, wet areas, windows and sealants. Leaks undo finishes.
3. **Does the layout work for the way you live?** If it does, a refit can deliver most of what you want. If it does not, you are in full-renovation territory, with structural advice and approvals.
4. **How long will you keep the villa?** A short hold argues for doing less, well. A long hold argues for renewing what is ageing while the house is open.

If the answers point to the structure, the services and the layout all at once, the question may no longer be which renovation but whether to renovate at all. Our guide to [renovating or rebuilding](/insights/renovate-or-rebuild-villa-dubai/) works through that decision.

## What does each level cost?

No official body publishes renovation rates in Dubai. A Dubai renovation contractor's 2026 guide, which cites no sources for its figures, gives about AED 120–250 per sq ft for basic work, AED 250–450 for mid-range work such as kitchens, bathrooms and interior redesign, and AED 450 or more for luxury and structural work ([renovation contractor's guide](https://smartrenovation.ae/how-much-does-villa-renovation-cost-in-dubai-full-price-breakdown-2026/)). Our [villa renovation cost guide](/insights/villa-renovation-cost-dubai/) compares these with other published figures and explains why they disagree.

The bands map roughly onto refresh, refit and full renovation, with one caution: a villa's tier is set less by the finishes you choose than by what the building needs. A sound house can be refreshed at the bottom of a band; the same house with failed waterproofing and end-of-life cooling is a different project, whatever the finishes schedule says.

## The half-way house to avoid

The most expensive outcome is a refit that should have been a full renovation: new kitchens and bathrooms installed in front of old pipes, old wiring and tired waterproofing. When those fail, the new finishes come out to reach them. If the survey says the services are close to the end of their life, either renew them now, while the walls are open, or plan the refit so they can be renewed later without undoing it.

## A view from the builder's side

On our side of the table, the projects that grew out of control usually started as refits. The owner asked for new kitchens and bathrooms; the strip-out found drainage that had failed, wiring that would not pass inspection and waterproofing that had stopped working. Each discovery was reasonable on its own, and each was priced as a variation, with the crew waiting. The fix was never better negotiation. It was opening up a little before pricing, so that the owner chose the level of renovation knowingly instead of having it chosen for them by the building.
