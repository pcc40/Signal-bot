After handover, a UAE villa is protected in two different ways. The contractual defects liability period, whose length your contract sets, covers ordinary defects such as leaks, failed seals and under-performing systems. Separately, the Civil Transactions Law makes the contractor and the engineer jointly liable for ten years for collapse and for defects that threaten the building's stability or safety. The first is where most owners' problems are found and fixed; the second is a backstop for the serious failures.

This guide explains both, how retention is released, what to test during the first twelve months, how to grade defects, and how to notify them. It does not give legal advice: on time limits and how the law applies to your contract, take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer.

## What is the defects liability period?

The [defects liability period](/glossary/#defects-liability-period) is a stretch of time after handover, set by your construction contract, during which the contractor must return to put right defects that appear. FIDIC's 2017 forms call it the defects notification period. Its length is a contractual choice, not a statutory one. Commentary on UAE practice gives twelve months for building works as an example, with mechanical and electrical works usually given longer ([Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/retention-final-account-uae-construction)).

It usually starts at handover, which in the contract may be called taking over or practical completion. In Dubai that follows the authority's completion inspection and completion certificate ([Dubai Municipality](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2020/06/Residential-Buildings_Building-Permits-Procedures.pdf)). Check three things in your contract before you accept the keys: how long the period is, whether different parts of the work have different periods, and what notice you must give to require the contractor to return.

If you are still at the snagging stage, start with our [villa snagging checklist](/insights/villa-snagging-checklist-dubai/). Anything you record and agree at handover should not need to wait for the defects period.

## What is decennial liability in the UAE?

[Decennial liability](/glossary/#decennial-liability) is the UAE's statutory ten-year liability for serious building failure. Under the 1985 Civil Transactions Law ([official text](https://www.dm.gov.ae/documents/federal-law-no-5-of-1985-concerning-the-issuance-of-the-civil-transactions-law-of-the-united-arab-emirates/)):

- **Article 880** makes the contractor and the engineer jointly liable for ten years for total or partial collapse of the building, and for defects that threaten its stability or safety. The period runs from delivery of the work, which in practice is usually the date in the taking-over certificate ([Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/decennial-liability-uae-contractors-engineers-2026)).
- **Article 881** limits an engineer who only designed, and did not follow the work on site, to liability for defects in the design.
- **Article 882** makes void any clause that tries to exclude or limit this liability. The parties can agree a longer period, not a shorter one.
- **Article 883** requires a claim to be brought within three years of the collapse or the discovery of the defect. The clock runs from discovery, not from handover.

### Did the 2026 Civil Transactions Law replace these articles?

Yes. Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 repealed and replaced the 1985 law with effect from 1 June 2026. It does not generally affect transactions concluded before that date unless it expressly says so ([Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/the-uaes-new-civil-code-explained-practical-changes-for-businesses-and-investors/)). So a villa contract signed before June 2026 is generally still governed by Articles 880 to 883.

For contracts made from 1 June 2026, commentary says the new law largely keeps the ten-year regime, now in Articles 821 to 824. It refers to the "engineer" rather than the "architect", clarifies joint and several liability, and preserves the contractor's right of recourse against subcontractors and suppliers ([Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/decennial-liability-uae-contractors-engineers-2026)). Other commentary notes that the new law reaffirms joint liability for collapse and expressly keeps decennial rules out of the relationship between the main contractor and its subcontractors ([Chambers and Partners](https://chambers.com/articles/construction-contracts-under-the-uae-2025-civil-transactions-law-what-has-changed)). Commentary does not yet settle every detail, including how the three-year claim limit carries over, so check the current text with a UAE-qualified lawyer.

### Villas bought from a developer

If you bought a completed villa from a developer in a jointly owned property in Dubai, Article 40 of Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 adds a further layer. The developer is liable for structural defects for ten years from the completion certificate, and for defective mechanical, electrical, sanitary and sewerage installations for one year from handover of the unit ([Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2019/Law%20No.%20%286%29%20of%202019%20Concerning%20Ownership%20of%20Jointly%20Owned%20Real%20Property%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html)). Whether that law applies to your villa depends on how the community is set up; a lawyer can tell you.

| Protection | What it covers | How long | Runs from | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contractual defects liability period | Ordinary defects in the work | As your contract says; often twelve months for building works | Handover, as your contract defines it | Your construction contract |
| Decennial liability | Collapse; defects threatening stability or safety | 10 years | Delivery of the work | Civil Transactions Law 1985, arts 880–883; new law arts 821–824 for contracts from 1 June 2026 |
| Claim under decennial liability | Bringing the claim | 3 years | Collapse or discovery of the defect | Art. 883 (1985 law) |
| Developer, jointly owned property (Dubai) | Structural defects; installations | 10 years; 1 year | Completion certificate; handover of the unit | Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019, art. 40 |
| Manufacturer and specialist warranties | Specific products and systems | As each warranty says | As each warranty says | The warranty documents |

## How is retention released?

[Retention](/glossary/#retention) is money deducted from each payment to the contractor and held as security for defects. RICS describes the general principle: a portion is released when the works are completed, and the remainder when the defects period has expired and the contract's certificate confirms it ([RICS GN 90/2012](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/retention_1st_edition_rics.pdf)).

FIDIC's 2017 forms follow that pattern. Under Sub-Clause 14.9, half of the retention is released when the taking-over certificate is issued and the other half after the last defects notification period expires. The performance certificate follows within 28 days of the end of that period, and the final statement after it; commentary warns that claims left out of the final statement can be lost ([Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/retention-final-account-uae-construction)). Some contracts allow a bank guarantee in place of cash retention.

The practical point is simple. Once the second half of the retention has gone, your leverage on any open defect is a legal claim rather than a payment. Book the end-of-period inspection several weeks before the period ends, so there is time to list, notify and re-inspect.

## What should you test in the first twelve months?

The first year is when a villa meets conditions that were not present at handover. A house handed over in the spring has never run its cooling through a Dubai summer; a house handed over in the summer has never seen heavy rain. Plan the year around those tests.

| When | What to test | What you are looking for |
|---|---|---|
| First month | Every wet area in daily use: showers, baths, basins, kitchen, laundry | Slow drains, staining on ceilings below, damp at skirtings, loose or hollow tiles |
| First month | Water tests on terraces, balconies and roofs, if not recorded before handover | Water inside at thresholds, sills and corners; ponding on flat roofs |
| July and August | Air conditioning under full summer load, every unit running for a full day | Rooms that do not hold their set temperature, condensate leaks from ceilings, noisy outdoor units |
| After the first heavy rain | Roofs, gutters, outlets, terraces, basement and site drainage | Blocked outlets, water tracking towards thresholds, damp in basements, standing water in the garden |
| Through the year | Pool level, plant room and pumps | A falling water level, pump noise, leaks in the plant room |
| Through the year | Façade sealants, stone and render after a season of heat | Open joints, hairline cracks, staining below sills and copings |
| Month 10 or 11 | A full re-inspection of the whole villa | Everything above, plus anything the household has noticed |

Rain deserves a plan of its own. In April 2024 more than 250 mm fell in under 24 hours in one area of Al Ain, and the National Centre of Meteorology described the event as exceptional in the UAE's climate record ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-witnesses-record-breaking-rains-highest-in-75-years)). Heavy rain is infrequent, so a roof or terrace detail that fails may not show it until the first storm. When it rains, walk the house that day, photograph what you find, and notify it.

## How serious is each defect?

Grade every defect when you report it. The grade tells the contractor what to attend to first and tells you what to hold back. This is our working classification, consistent with the one in our [snagging checklist](/guides/villa-snagging-checklist/); it is not a legal category, and your contract decides the contractor's obligations.

| Grade | Villa examples | What to ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Cracking in structural elements; water coming through a roof, terrace or basement; an electrical safety fault; an insecure balustrade | Immediate make-safe, investigation of the cause, a remedy agreed in writing with your registered consultant, and a joint re-inspection. If it may threaten stability or safety, take legal advice early, because decennial liability may be relevant |
| Major | AC that cannot hold temperature in summer; wrong falls to drains or terraces; a pool losing water; failing glazing seals | A written date for the repair, the cause identified rather than the symptom treated, and a re-test under the same conditions |
| Minor | Cracked grout, sticking doors, sealant shrinkage, small cracks in paint and plaster from drying out | Logged, photographed and batched for a single return visit, closed before the end-of-period inspection |

## How should you notify defects?

Follow the notice clause in your contract to the letter: who notices go to, in what form, and within what time. As a general practice:

- Notify in writing, to the address the contract names, with a copy to your registered consultant.
- Keep one numbered defects log for the whole villa, with the date found, a photograph, the location, the grade and the date notified.
- Give the contractor access to inspect and repair; most contracts give the contractor the first opportunity to put defects right.
- Do not engage others to repair a defect at the contractor's cost without checking the contract and taking advice; a misstep can weaken your claim.
- Keep records of every repair visit and re-test.

On time limits, two things are worth knowing. Contractual notices can have short deadlines, and statutory periods run independently of the contract. Take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer before any deadline approaches, not after.

## The builder's side of the defects period

We ran the contractor's side of defects periods for two decades. Here is what we saw.

By the time the defects period starts, the contractor's site team has usually moved to the next job and the subcontractors have been paid. Every return visit is a cost with no new income attached. On our side, the visits that happened quickly were the ones backed by a clear, graded list and a meaningful sum still held. A stream of individual emails, each describing one item loosely, tended to wait until there were enough items to justify sending a trade.

The end-of-period inspection was always the one that mattered. A client who walked the house properly in month eleven, with a full list, got it closed. A client who called in month thirteen, after the retention had gone, usually got a polite reply and a long wait.
