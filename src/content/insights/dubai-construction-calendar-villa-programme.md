A villa programme in Dubai has to carry three known interruptions: reduced working hours in Ramadan, the Eid holidays, and the midday break on outdoor work from 15 June to 15 September. None of them is a surprise, and none of them stops a build. They become a problem when a pour, a delivery or an approval lands in the wrong week and nobody planned for it.

This guide sets out what each one changes, the 2027 dates to plan around, and what we would ask a contractor to show before you sign.

## What changes on site during Ramadan?

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation announced on 12 February 2026 that private-sector working hours are reduced by two hours a day during Ramadan. Companies may use flexible working or remote work within those reduced hours, depending on the nature of the work. The decision implements the Implementing Regulations of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on employment relationships.

A site cannot work remotely, so the practical effect is a shorter productive day for the contractor and for everyone the contractor depends on: consultants, suppliers and subcontractors. Two things follow for an owner.

- **Output falls and the shape of the day changes.** Heavy work is usually pulled towards the morning. Anything that needs a late-day inspection, a delivery slot or a decision from your consultant can slip by a day.
- **The month is not lost, it is thinner.** In our experience the sensible response is to plan Ramadan for work that does not depend on many outside parties: internal finishes, joinery installation, second fix, snagging. Avoid placing a critical structural pour, a long-lead delivery or a major approval milestone inside it.

The Ramadan hours are a general employment rule. We have not found a published rule that fixes site hours for private villa projects separately, so how a contractor and your community manage access and noise in the month is something to ask for in writing, not assume.

## When are Ramadan and Eid in 2027?

Islamic months run about eleven days earlier each year, so the whole block moves through the calendar. The dates are confirmed by moon sighting, so until the UAE announces them, every date below is an estimate.

| Event | Likely date (2027) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New Year's Day | Friday 1 January | One public holiday day |
| Ramadan begins | About Monday 8 February | Astronomy Centre estimate; ends 8 or 9 March |
| Eid Al Fitr | 9 or 10 March | Holiday of three or four days, with a possible extra day if the crescent is not seen |
| Eid Al Adha | Around 15 to 18 May | Follows the Day of Arafah; depends on moon sighting |
| Islamic New Year | Around 6 June | Depends on moon sighting |
| Midday break | 15 June to 15 September | Fixed dates, every year |
| Prophet's birthday | Expected 14 August | Reported as an expected date |

Sources for the estimates are Khaleej Times (Astronomy Centre dates) and The National (expected holiday dates). The practical point is that in 2027 Ramadan, Eid Al Fitr and Eid Al Adha all fall before the summer break begins, so the first half of the year carries three interruptions and the second half carries one long one. The same block arrives roughly eleven days earlier each year, so a build that spans two years will meet each of them twice.

## What is the midday break and what does it cost a villa site?

From 15 June to 15 September every year, work under direct sunlight and in open areas is banned from 12:30pm to 3pm. 2026 was the 22nd consecutive year.

The rules matter to an owner for three reasons:

- **Penalties are real.** Khaleej Times reports a fine of AED 5,000 per worker, capped at AED 50,000 where several workers are involved. Depending on severity and frequency, a contractor can also face suspension of its establishment file and a downgrade of its tier classification with the Ministry, and cases involving injury can be referred to prosecution.
- **Exemptions are narrow.** MoHRE describes them as covering work that cannot be interrupted for technical, operational or public-safety reasons. A contractor who says it will work through the break on your villa needs to explain which activity it is claiming exemption for and why.
- **Employer duties apply to everyone.** Shaded rest areas, cold drinking water, cooling equipment and first-aid supplies are required on site. On a visit, they are easy to check.

For the programme, the three months do not stop work, they change what is done when. The usual response is early starts and moving internal trades indoors during the hottest weeks. What it makes harder is structural and external work: concrete, waterproofing, roof finishes, paving and landscaping. A programme that shows the same weekly progress on those activities in August as in January has not been thought through. Our guide to [how long a villa takes to build](/insights/how-long-to-build-a-villa-dubai/) explains how the summer fits into the overall duration.

## How should you build the calendar into the programme?

On the contractor's side, the first thing we did on any job was lay the shutdown weeks and the weather risk over the programme and ask what was sitting on top of them. In the UK it was the Christmas break and the winter. Here the equivalents are Ramadan, the two Eids and the summer. The exercise is the same: mark the dead weeks, then move the critical work out of them.

| What to place | Where it fits best | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Structural pours, waterproofing, external works | Outside Ramadan, the Eids and, where possible, 15 June to 15 September | Weather, curing and access need full working days |
| Long-lead orders (glazing, stone, lifts, specialist plant) | Ordered early enough to arrive ahead of a holiday block | A delivery that lands in Eid week usually waits for the next working day |
| Approval and inspection requests | Submitted early enough that the response is not due in a holiday week | Authority and developer response times are not within your contractor's control |
| Owner decisions and sign-offs | Before the block, not during it | Late decisions are the owner's delay and the owner's cost |
| Internal finishes, second fix, snagging | Ramadan and the summer weeks | Less dependent on outside parties and direct sun |

These are our own planning rules of thumb, not published benchmarks. They are most useful when they are written into the programme at tender, so that the contractor's dates already allow for the calendar.

## What should the contract say about holidays and time?

Public holidays, Ramadan hours and the midday break are all known in advance, which is why a contractor should price and programme for them. When delay claims arise later, the contract's definition of working days and of the events that justify more time decides the argument. Four points are worth settling before signature:

1. **Define working days.** State which days count, and say that known public holidays and Ramadan hours are allowed for in the programme.
2. **Ask for a calendar-aware programme.** The tender programme should show the Ramadan window, each Eid and the summer break against the critical activities, with the contractor's plan for each.
3. **Name the events that justify more time.** Check which events entitle the contractor to an extension, and that a foreseeable holiday is not quietly one of them. Take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer on the wording; our guide to [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/) covers the main clauses.
4. **Agree how approvals are tracked.** If an approval is late because of a holiday, the record should show when it was requested and whether the request was complete.

## What should you do as the owner?

Check the programme for collisions before the contractor mobilises, and again at each milestone. Book your own site visits and decision dates outside the dead weeks, and keep a record of when you gave each decision. The [owner site visits guide](/insights/owner-site-visits-villa-build/) explains what to check at each stage. If you live overseas, a holiday week is also when a site goes quiet without anyone telling you, so ask for a short written progress note before and after each block.
