A villa renovation in Dubai typically costs from about AED 200,000–350,000 for a basic refresh to AED 400,000–800,000 for mid-range work and well over AED 1 million for a luxury or structural refurbishment, according to published 2026 guides. Per square foot, that is roughly AED 120–250 for basic work, AED 250–450 for mid-range and AED 450 or more for luxury. Those are mostly contractor prices; design, approvals, contingency and the cost of living elsewhere sit on top.

The published ranges are a useful starting point. What they cannot tell you is how much of your villa's real cost is still hidden behind its walls. That is where renovation budgets are won or lost, and it is what most of this guide is about.

## How much does a villa renovation cost in Dubai in 2026?

Two published sources set out the market clearly, one by scope and one by villa type.

| Scope or villa type | Published range (AED) | Per sq ft (AED) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic: painting, flooring, small upgrades | 200,000–350,000 | 120–250 | Smart Renovation (2026) |
| Mid-range: kitchen, bathrooms, interior redesign | 400,000–800,000 | 250–450 | Smart Renovation (2026) |
| Luxury: premium materials, structural change | 1,000,000+ | 450+ | Smart Renovation (2026) |
| Three-bedroom townhouse: interior, kitchen and bathrooms | 250,000–400,000 | — | Property Finder (Sep 2025) |
| Four-bedroom independent villa: interior and landscaping | 600,000–850,000 | — | Property Finder (Sep 2025) |
| Five-bedroom older villa: full upgrade with smart systems | 900,000–1,400,000 | — | Property Finder (Sep 2025) |
| Luxury villa: structural work, premium materials | 1,500,000–3,500,000+ | — | Property Finder (Sep 2025) |

At room level, the sources disagree more. Smart Renovation puts a kitchen at AED 80,000–200,000 and a bathroom at AED 30,000–80,000 each. Property Finder gives lower starting points: kitchens from AED 25,000 and bathrooms from about AED 12,000 for standard fittings, around AED 20,000 for premium. The gap is the difference between replacing units and rebuilding a room, with new drainage, waterproofing, electrics and ventilation behind it.

Two cautions. These are averages across very different houses, and a renovation's tier is decided less by the finishes you choose than by what the existing building needs. A villa with sound roofs and healthy services can be refreshed at the bottom of a band. The same villa with failed waterproofing and end-of-life air conditioning is a different project, whatever the finishes schedule says.

## What is not included in a renovation quote?

Most published figures, and most contractor quotes, cover the building work itself. Outside that, owners usually need to budget for:

- **Design and engineering.** Any renovation that touches structure, layout, services or the façade needs drawings from a DM-registered consultant. Real Estate Club Dubai puts multi-discipline design fees on new villas at 6–12% of construction cost; we use the same range as a guide for a full refurbishment.
- **Approvals.** A master developer NOC where your community has one, then a permit from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits. Published fee figures for renovation permits vary widely, so ask your consultant to set out the actual charges for your scope.
- **Contingency.** Real Estate Club Dubai suggests 10–15% on new builds. A renovation carries more unknowns, not fewer.
- **External works.** Smart Renovation puts outdoor, pool and landscaping work at AED 50,000–200,000 combined; a pool rebuild alone can use much of that.
- **Furniture, AV, lighting control and automation**, which are often outside the main contract.
- **Living elsewhere.** Rent, storage and removals if you move out for the works.
- **Inflation.** Currie & Brown forecast UAE construction cost inflation of around 3% for 2026, as cited by PRASOON.

Always read the contractor's exclusions before you compare totals. On a renovation, the exclusions list often says more about the price than the price does.

## What are the three hidden costs in older villas?

Our founder, Philip Chapman, ran P Chapman Construction, a premium residential builder in the UK, for 21 years. From the builder's side, the renovation budgets that failed almost never failed on the rates. They failed on three things the original price did not capture.

### 1. What is behind the finishes

A new build is priced from drawings. A renovation is priced from what can be seen, and in an older villa the expensive parts usually cannot be.

- **Waterproofing.** Roof membranes, terraces over rooms, wet-room tanking and basements all have a finite life. Failure shows late, as staining, blistering paint or a musty room, long after the damage started. Replacing a membrane means stripping finishes and often lifting screeds, before any new interior goes in.
- **Services.** Air conditioning, ductwork, pipework and electrical distribution installed when the villa was built may be near the end of its life, or undersized for how you want to live now. In Dubai the cooling system is the heart of the house, and replacing it is a building project, not a fitting.
- **Concrete and salt.** In coastal communities, wind-borne salt attacks metalwork, fixings, frames and, where concrete cover is poor, reinforcement. Cracking and rust staining need a structural engineer's view, not paint.

On the contractor's side, the first thing we priced on any refurbishment was the risk in the envelope and the services. Interiors are predictable; roofs, basements and plant are not. When the information was thin, we priced what we could see and knew the rest would come back as variations, priced by one firm with no competition. That is the single biggest reason renovation budgets move.

### 2. The knock-on work

Renovations spread. Move a bathroom and you need new drainage falls, new waterproofing and new screeds. Open up a wall and the electrics behind it no longer suit the new layout. Replace the air-conditioning units and the ducts, controls, condensate routes and electrical supply may all need to follow. Lay new flooring on one level and the thresholds, doors and stairs no longer line up.

There is also the regulatory knock-on. The Dubai Building Code applies to building designs permitted after it came into force (Decree No. 45 of 2021), and the code itself describes its application to the renovation, modification and addition of existing buildings. In practice, new work in an older villa is designed to today's rules, not the rules the house was built under. Your consultant will tell you what that means for your scope; it is better to know at concept stage than at permit stage.

In our experience, a brief that starts as "kitchen, bathrooms and finishes" and ends as "most of the services and half the screeds" is the norm in older villas, not the exception. Price the knock-ons at design stage and they are decisions. Discover them on site and they are variations.

### 3. The cost of time

Every extra month on a renovation costs you three ways. The contractor's preliminaries, the cost of running the site, continue: Turner & Townsend put preliminaries in Dubai at 12–14% of contract value (cited by PRASOON). Your rent and storage continue if you have moved out. And the house is unusable for another month.

Time is lost in predictable places: discoveries that need redesign, late decisions on finishes, design changes after the NOC that need resubmitting, and long-lead items such as glazing, joinery and imported stone ordered too late. Published guides put larger projects with structural work at six months or more on site, before approvals.

Living in the villa during the works rarely saves money once services, waterproofing or structure are involved. The contractor has to phase the job around you, protect occupied rooms and work slower, and you pay for all of it in preliminaries.

## How do you keep a villa renovation on budget?

- **Survey before you design.** Commission a proper condition survey of roofs, wet areas, basement, pool and MEP, and open up small areas where it matters. It is the cheapest money on the project.
- **Price the design at concept stage.** Get an early cost check before the drawings are finished, so the brief and budget can be adjusted while it is cheap to do so.
- **Turn provisional sums into scope.** If the bid carries an allowance for "waterproofing repairs" or "MEP remedials", the real cost is still unknown. Investigate before signing, or at least agree rates.
- **Compare bids like-for-like.** Normalise exclusions, allowances and preliminaries before looking at totals. Our guide to [choosing a villa contractor in Dubai](/insights/how-to-choose-a-villa-contractor-dubai/) explains how.
- **Hold the contingency yourself.** Release it by decision, not by default.
- **Price every change before it is instructed.** Cost and time, in writing, first.
- **Decide early, order early.** Finishes and long-lead items fixed before work starts protect the programme.
- **Read the contract.** See [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/), and take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer.

If the scope keeps growing towards new structure, a new envelope and full services replacement, stop and price a rebuild properly alongside it.
