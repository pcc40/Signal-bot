On a Dubai villa build, your registered consultant designs the house and carries the statutory technical role, your contractor builds it, and an owner's representative acts solely for you on cost, programme, contract and decisions. A "project manager" can be any of these things depending on who employs them, which is why the title matters less than whose interests the person serves. The roles complement each other; none replaces the consultant.

## What does each role actually do?

The confusion is understandable. In the UAE, "project manager", "owner's rep", "PMC" and "consultant" are used loosely, and firms describe themselves differently. Focus on function and on who pays them.

| | registered consultant | Project manager | Owner's representative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who appoints | Owner | Owner, consultant or contractor | Owner |
| Who they serve | Owner, within professional and statutory duties | Whoever appoints them | Owner only |
| Design | Yes, architecture, structure, MEP | No | No; reviews design against brief and budget |
| Authority submissions | Yes | No | No |
| Statutory technical role on site | Yes | No | No |
| Cost and programme control | Partly, varies by appointment | Usually | Yes, core role |
| Bid comparison and selection | Prepares bid package, advises | Sometimes | Helps the owner compare like-for-like and choose |
| Change and variation control | Reviews technically | Sometimes | Yes, commercially and contractually |
| Independent site visits and reporting | Reports on statutory matters | Varies | Yes, client-side |
| Stake in design or construction | Design fee | Depends on employer | None |

The consultant role is defined by law. Under Dubai Law No. 14 of 2025, only registered consultancies may practise engineering consultancy or present themselves as engineering consultants, and the law applies across Dubai including special development zones and free zones ([Law No. 14 of 2025](https://www.dm.gov.ae/documents/law-no-14-of-2025-regulating-engineering-consultancy-practice-in-the-emirate-of-dubai-municipality/), [Dubai Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/october/05-10/mohammed-bin-rashid)). The other two roles are defined by contract.

### The registered consultant

Your consultant is your designer and your statutory technical professional. They take the brief, design the villa, coordinate the engineering, prepare and submit the approval drawings, and normally carry the statutory site role during construction. They are essential. You cannot build without one. Dubai Municipality publishes standard consultancy contract forms for private villas, in Arabic, and the BPS app lists registered consultants with ratings.

### The project manager

"Project manager" describes an activity, not an allegiance. A contractor has project managers. Some consultants offer project management as an add-on. Some independent firms offer it to owners. The work is planning, coordinating, chasing and reporting. Before you rely on a project manager, ask who appoints them and who pays them.

### The owner's representative

An owner's representative manages the project from the owner's side of the table only. Typical scope: setting and controlling the budget, the programme and the decision schedule; helping the owner compare bids and choose; reviewing valuations and variations; independent client-side site visits and reporting; and keeping the consultant and contractor accountable to the brief. The owner's representative does not design, does not make authority submissions and does not take on the consultant's statutory role. The value lies in having no stake in the design fee or the construction price.

## Where are the conflicts of interest?

Every party on a villa build has a legitimate commercial interest. Conflicts arise when one party is asked to check its own work.

**Design-and-build firms.** One company designs, prices and builds. That can be efficient, with a single point of responsibility. But the same firm decides how much building the design contains, prices it, and then prices every change. Nobody independent is testing whether the design is efficient or the price is fair, unless you arrange it.

**Consultants checking their own design.** When the consultant who designed the villa also reviews the contractor's claims for design-related changes, they are judging, in part, their own drawings. Most consultants handle this professionally. But a variation caused by a design gap is a difficult thing for the designer to call. In August 2025, Dubai Municipality warned villa consultancies about over-designing structures and inflating build costs (Gulf News). A design that is heavier than it needs to be is expensive, and a second, independent look is the simplest way to test it.

**Fees tied to construction value.** Consultant fees in Dubai are commonly a percentage of construction cost. That is market practice, not wrongdoing, but it means the fee structure itself does not reward making the building cheaper.

**Contractor-employed project managers.** The contractor's project manager is there to deliver the contractor's job profitably. That is their job. It is not the same job as protecting your budget.

Anyone who has run a building company knows the pattern: projects where the client has no independent adviser are commercially easier for the contractor. Provisional sums go unquestioned, variation rates go untested and monthly valuations are rarely challenged. Nobody is doing anything wrong. There is simply no one asking.

## What should each appointment include?

Whatever you call the role, put it in writing. Take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer on the final form.

### The consultant's appointment

- Every design stage included, and which are excluded
- The statutory site role and how often the consultant attends site
- Authority and master-developer submissions, including resubmissions
- How design changes requested by the owner are charged
- Responsibility for coordinating specialist designers (pool, landscape, lighting, automation)
- Fee basis and what happens to the fee if construction cost changes
- Professional indemnity insurance

### The project manager's or owner's representative's appointment

- Who they act for, stated explicitly
- A clear scope: budget, programme, bid comparison, valuation and variation review, site visits, reporting frequency
- What they may and may not instruct on the owner's behalf; in our view, the owner should keep the authority to approve cost
- A confirmation that they have no financial relationship with the consultant, contractor or suppliers
- A fee basis linked to scope, not to construction value, where possible
- The interface with the consultant, so roles do not overlap or leave gaps

### The contractor's contract

Covered in our guide to [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/). The key point here: the contract should name who represents the owner and what that person may do.

## When do you need an owner's representative, and when don't you?

To be straightforward about it, not every villa needs one.

**You probably do not need one if** the villa is relatively simple, the design is complete before pricing, you have built before, you live in Dubai, you have time to attend site and read the monthly reports, and you trust your consultant to act firmly on your behalf with the contractor. In that case, a well-appointed consultant and a sound contract may be enough.

**You probably do need one if** any of the following apply:

- You live overseas or travel a lot. See [building a villa in Dubai from the UK](/insights/building-a-villa-in-dubai-from-the-uk/).
- The villa is large, bespoke or technically demanding: basements, waterfront plots, structural glazing, heavy automation.
- You are using a design-and-build firm.
- You have not built before and do not know what a valuation or variation should look like.
- The budget matters to you and you want someone with no stake in the price testing it.
- The project has stalled or is in dispute.

Fees are commonly estimated at roughly 2–5% of construction cost internationally, although PRASOON notes there is no credible published benchmark for Dubai. The test is simple: would independent control of cost, programme and variations save more than it costs? On a straightforward villa, it may not. On a complex one, in our experience, it usually does.

## What should you ask before appointing an owner's representative?

If you decide you want one, test the fit before you sign. These are the questions we would ask in your position:

- **Who else pays you on this project?** The only acceptable answer is nobody. Ask whether they receive referral fees, commissions or any other benefit from consultants, contractors or suppliers.
- **What have you built, and from which side?** Experience on the contractor's side, the consultant's side or the developer's side gives different strengths. Ask for specifics, and be wary of anyone who claims experience they cannot describe in detail.
- **What exactly will you deliver each month?** A sample report tells you more than a brochure.
- **How often will you be on site, and who will come?** Agree the frequency and the person, not just the firm.
- **What will you not do?** A good owner's representative is clear about the boundary with your registered consultant, and will not claim to design, make authority submissions or take on the consultant's statutory role.
- **How is the fee structured?** A fixed or time-based fee against a defined scope keeps incentives cleaner than a percentage of construction value.

An honest answer to the last question often tells you more than the others.
