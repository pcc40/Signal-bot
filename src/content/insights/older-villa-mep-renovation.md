In an older Dubai villa, the wiring, water pipes, drains and air conditioning are where most of a renovation's surprises sit, because they are hidden behind finishes until the strip-out. The way to control them is to survey and open up before you tender, decide what you will replace rather than repair, and make sure the contractor prices what nobody can see in a way you can check.

This article covers what we expect to find, how to find it early, and how contractors price the unknown. For the wider picture, start with our guide on [how to plan a villa renovation in Dubai](/insights/how-to-plan-a-villa-renovation-dubai/).

## Why MEP is the biggest unknown in an older villa

Mechanical, electrical and plumbing work (MEP) sits in walls, ceilings, floor screeds and the ground. Finishes, kitchens and joinery are visible and easy to price; services are not. A contractor quoting from a walk-round sees sockets, taps and air-conditioning grilles, not the cable behind them, the joints in the pipes or the fall of the drains.

Age matters for a regulatory reason too. Dubai Municipality made its green building regulations mandatory for government buildings in January 2011 and for all new buildings in March 2014. Circular No. 183 of 2011 required solar water heating on new single-owner buildings, including private villas, from 2012. The Dubai Building Code, which unified design rules previously spread across Dubai Municipality, the Dubai Development Authority and Trakhees, came into force at the end of 2021. A villa permitted before these dates was designed to the rules of its time. When you replace its services wholesale, your consultant will tell you which current requirements the new work must meet, so a full MEP replacement is often a redesign rather than a like-for-like swap.

On the contractor's side, the first thing we priced on any old house was the services, because they decided whether the job made money. A tidy kitchen quote is worth little if the first day of strip-out finds a corroded pipe run behind it.

## What we typically find behind the walls

Every villa is different, and only a survey tells you about yours. These are the items we look at first in an older villa, what tends to show the problem and how a contractor will usually want to price it.

| System | What we check | What tends to show a problem | How it is usually priced if found |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical distribution | Age and condition of distribution boards; protective devices; labelling | Heat marks, loose terminations, missing labels, boards with no spare ways | Lump sum once surveyed; schedule of rates for extra circuits |
| Wiring | Test results; cable condition at accessories; added circuits | Poor test results, brittle insulation, extensions and junctions buried in walls | Provisional sum for a rewire, or rate per point |
| Water supply pipes | Pipe material; pressure test; joints and valves | Pressure loss, discoloured water, corroded or seized valves, repeat leaks | Rate per metre and per fitting, or a provisional sum |
| Water tanks and pumps | Tank condition, access, pump age | Sediment, a poorly sealed lid, a noisy or cycling pump | Fixed price for replacement |
| Drainage | CCTV camera survey; falls; gully and inspection chamber condition | Root ingress, displaced joints, standing water, recurring smells | Provisional sum until the survey is done |
| Hot water | Heater type and age; solar thermal if present | Scale, corrosion, poor recovery, no isolation | Fixed price per unit |
| Air conditioning | Plant age and condition; duct condition and insulation; condensate drains | Poor cooling in some rooms, wet ceilings near units, damaged duct insulation | Fixed price for plant; rates for ductwork |
| Wet-area waterproofing | Behind showers and over slabs | Staining in rooms below, loose tiles, hollow screeds | Provisional sum until opened up |

Two patterns repeat. First, earlier owners add circuits, sockets and air-conditioning units over the years, often without updating drawings, so the record is wrong before anyone starts. Second, services fail at the joints and edges: where pipes pass through walls, where drains connect to chambers and where cables terminate. That is where to open up.

## How to survey before you tender

The aim is to turn unknowns into priced items before the contract is signed. In our experience a few days of investigation repays itself many times over.

1. **Get the drawings.** Ask the developer or the community management office for the original approved drawings and any later NOCs. They show what was approved, which is not always what was built, but they tell you where to look.
2. **Electrical testing.** Have a qualified electrical contractor inspect and test the installation and report in writing on its condition, the distribution boards and the capacity of the existing supply.
3. **Water pressure test.** A pressure test on the supply pipes shows leaks you cannot see. Ask for the pipe materials and any corroded valves to be recorded.
4. **CCTV drain survey.** A camera survey of the drains, with a short video and a marked-up plan, shows root ingress, displaced joints and poor falls.
5. **Air-conditioning inspection.** Record the age and condition of each unit, the condition of the ducts and their insulation, and where the condensate drains run.
6. **Targeted opening up.** Agree with the owner a handful of spots to open: behind one shower, one section of ceiling under a bathroom, the kitchen sink wall. Make good afterwards.

Then write the findings into the scope. Our [renovation project management](/services/villa-renovation-project-management/) work includes coordinating this survey with the owner's consultant and contractor, and turning it into a scope that bidders can price on the same basis.

## Repair, replace or redesign?

Once the survey is in, each system falls into one of three decisions. The table shows how we think about them.

| Decision | When it makes sense | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Repair | Tests are good; the layout is not changing; the system has years of life left | Repairs behind new finishes are hard to reach later; record them on drawings |
| Replace like-for-like | Condition is poor but the layout and loads are unchanged | Your consultant confirms which current requirements the new work must meet |
| Redesign | New kitchen, more bathrooms, new air-conditioning layout, pool, EV charging, an extension | Needs the consultant's MEP design, the developer's approval and possibly an authority permit; check the supply capacity early |

If the new loads need more capacity than the existing electricity supply provides, the change to the supply goes through DEWA, which offers an electricity network modification service. Your consultant manages that application; we make sure it is on the programme, because a supply upgrade on a finished villa is the kind of item that holds up a handover.

## How contractors price what they cannot see

A contractor who cannot see the services has three ways to deal with the risk:

- **A provisional sum.** A placeholder amount for work that cannot be defined yet, adjusted to the actual cost when it is known. It is transparent, but only if the contract says how the actual cost is measured and approved. Our article on [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/) covers the clauses to look for.
- **A schedule of rates.** Agreed prices per metre of pipe, per electrical point or per drain repair, applied to whatever is found. This works well for MEP because the quantities are uncertain but the type of work is predictable.
- **A hidden risk allowance.** A lump sum loaded into the price. The owner pays for the risk whether or not it happens, and the contractor has every reason to argue that a discovery falls outside it.

When we [compare bids like-for-like](/services/consultant-and-contractor-selection/#quote-review), MEP is where quotes differ most. One contractor includes a full rewire; another prices only "make safe and test"; a third excludes drainage altogether. Lining them up against the same survey-based scope is the only fair comparison.

Published cost guides rarely separate out MEP. Property Finder, for example, groups plumbing with painting and tiling in its standard villa band and lists permit fees from AED 500 to AED 15,000 depending on the type of work. Our article on [villa renovation costs in Dubai](/insights/villa-renovation-cost-dubai/) sets out the published ranges and what they leave out.

## Approvals and community rules

In master-planned communities the developer's approval comes first. Emaar's guidance for Arabian Ranches, for example, says any alteration to the villa has to be approved by Emaar through the community portal, that interior painting and regular maintenance do not need a NOC, and that contractors need an entry permit to work in the community. Nakheel communities follow a similar route, with the Nakheel NOC before any authority permit; see our article on the [Nakheel NOC for villa renovations](/insights/nakheel-noc-villa-renovation/).

Whether the MEP work also needs a building permit from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, is a question for your DM-registered consultant. Replacing a tap is maintenance; relocating bathrooms, adding air-conditioning plant or changing the electricity supply generally is not. Do not fix the contractor's start date until the answer is in writing.

## Sequencing MEP in a renovation

MEP sits in the middle of the programme and drives everything around it:

- **Strip-out and opening up** confirm the survey. Allow a short hold point here to price any discoveries before work continues.
- **First fix** (cables, pipes, ducts and drains before walls and ceilings close) is the last chance to see the services. Our [site visits and reporting](/services/site-visits-and-reporting/) include an owner's visit before close-up, with photographs of every wall and ceiling so you know where everything runs.
- **Testing and commissioning** before finishes: electrical test certificates, pressure tests and air-conditioning commissioning records should be in the handover file.
- **Second fix and finishes** follow, then [snagging and handover](/services/snagging-and-handover/).

Long-lead items matter here too. Air-conditioning plant, distribution boards and water heaters can set the date more than the site work does; our [villa renovation timeline](/insights/villa-renovation-timeline-dubai/) shows where they fit.
