Three authorities issue villa building permits in Dubai: Dubai Municipality, Trakhees and the Dubai Development Authority (DDA). Which one covers your villa is decided by the plot, not by the community name, so the only safe answer is the one your registered consultant confirms from your plot documents. In Abu Dhabi the route is easier to name: the municipality issues the permit through TAMM, under rules set by the Department of Municipalities and Transport (DMT).

That sounds like a technicality. It is not. The authority decides which regulations the design is drawn to, which portal the application goes through, who inspects the work and who issues the completion certificate. Get it wrong at the start and the design is reworked later, which is where months go.

## Why can't I tell the authority from the community name?

Because Dubai law draws the boundaries plot by plot, and they move.

- **The DDA.** Law No. 8 of 2023 defines the DDA's free zone as the Creative Clusters plus specific land plots whose boundaries are marked on plans attached to the law, and any other zone or plot the Ruler later places under the DDA ([Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2023/Law%20No.%20(8)%20of%202023%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20(15)%20of%202014.pdf)). The plans list individual plots by number and district. The DDA describes its own role as permitting construction activities, carrying out inspections and issuing completion certificates for projects within its jurisdiction ([DDA](https://dda.gov.ae)).
- **Trakhees.** Decree No. 22 of 2009 created special development zones covering land owned by the Ports, Customs and Free Zone Corporation (PCFC) and Dubai World and their affiliated companies, naming Nakheel among them, and gave PCFC exclusive responsibility for licensing there ([Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Decree%2022%20of%202009.html)). Trakhees is the PCFC body that regulates those zones. In March 2024 Nakheel and Meydan were folded into Dubai Holding ([Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/march/16-03/mohammed-bin-rashid-mandates-incorporation-of-nakheel-and-meydan)), a reminder that ownership, and the arrangements that follow it, change.
- **Dubai Municipality.** Under the 2023 urban planning law, the authority responsible for a special development zone or free zone issues planning permits for the land it oversees, while Dubai Municipality holds the wider planning powers across the emirate ([Dubai Legislation Portal](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2023/Law%20No.%20(16)%20of%202023%20Concerning%20Urban%20Planning.html)).

So a single community can, in principle, contain plots that sit differently, and a community's position can change by legislation. That is why our rule is simple: **confirm with your consultant; never assume by community.**

## Does it matter which authority it is if everything goes through one window?

Less than it used to, but yes. Since 2022, building permits for Dubai Municipality, the DDA, Trakhees and the Dubai Integrated Economic Zones Authority have run through a single unified e-window, with Civil Defence, RTA, DEWA and the telecoms providers linked in ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-launches-single-window-for-building-permits/)). The [Build in Dubai](https://buildindubai.gov.ae/) platform now covers a project from land and design to completion and later alterations.

One front door does not mean one rulebook. Each authority still applies its own regulations, fees, inspection regime and completion process. For an owner, the practical questions are:

- Which regulations is the design being drawn to?
- Is the consultant experienced with this authority, and is the contractor registered to work under it?
- Which inspections will the authority carry out, and when do they fall in the programme?
- Who issues the completion certificate, and what does it need to see?

### Why the Dubai rows all say "confirm"

We could have filled the column with what agents, forums and listings say. Some of it is probably right. ## Why the master developer usually matters more than the authority

In practice, the approval that most often sets a villa programme is not the permit. It is the master developer's review.

In Nakheel communities, Nakheel's guidance is that you need an NOC from its Urban Planning team before making alterations, and that site access involves its own requirements, including a security deposit, a site access permit and a site fence ([Nakheel](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us)). Owners who skip that step find out later: Gulf News reported Jumeirah Islands owners being stopped from modifying villas without Nakheel's approval ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/uae/jumeirah-islands-owners-prevented-from-modifying-villas-without-approval-1.1172089)). Our [Nakheel NOC guide](/insights/nakheel-noc-villa-renovation/) sets out the sequence.

Abu Dhabi master communities work the same way. The development control regulations for Al Raha Gardens, an Aldar community, published in the Abu Dhabi Municipality's e-library, require an Abu Dhabi Municipality building permit, Abu Dhabi Distribution Company approval and Civil Defence approval for structural work, extensions and new structures, and require some finishes to be colour-matched to the existing for Aldar's approval ([Abu Dhabi Municipality](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/adm/-/media/Project/DMT/ADM/E-Library/ADM-Download-6-3.pdf)). Each community has its own regulations; get the ones for your plot.

## How is Abu Dhabi different?

Abu Dhabi is easier to name and has moved faster on simplification. Building permits are requested through TAMM ([TAMM](https://www.tamm.abudhabi/en/life-events/business/ManageyourPermitsMemberships/Constructions/RequestaNewBuildingPermit)), under regulations set by DMT. In September 2025 DMT amended the private villa regulations, including a 1.5-metre front setback and allowing a lightweight rooftop majlis ([DMT](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/en/Media-Centre/News/Department-of-Municipalities-and-Transport-Announces-Amendments-to-Private-Villas-Regulations)). In August 2025 it introduced a streamlined route for minor works such as small extensions, pools, canopies and fences, which licensed contractors can apply for directly ([DMT](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/en/Media-Centre/News/DMT-Unveils-Streamlined-Permit-Process-for-Minor-Works)).

On plot communities the master developer still comes first. Modon's Wadeem on Hudayriyat has more than 1,700 plots for owners to design their own villas ([Modon](https://www.modon.com/media-centre/details/2025/07/01/modon-launches-wadeem--its-first-residential-plots-communityon-hudayriyat-island)), and each owner works to Modon's plot requirements before the municipality permit. Our [Abu Dhabi permit guide](/insights/abu-dhabi-villa-permit-tamm/) covers the TAMM route in detail.

## What does the authority change for your programme?

The authority affects the programme in four practical ways.

1. **The design brief.** Setbacks, heights, basements and roof structures are drawn to the authority's regulations and the developer's guidelines together. Confirm both before the consultant starts concept design.
2. **The team.** Ask whether the consultant regularly submits to this authority and whether the contractor is registered to work under it. A contractor who has to register partway through is a contractor who is not on site.
3. **The inspection calendar.** Staged inspections fall at different points and are booked differently. Build them into the programme as hold points, not as surprises.
4. **The finish line.** The completion certificate and service connections come from the authority's process. Handover dates in the contract should follow it, not a hopeful date.

From the builder's side, this is familiar. On every UK job we ran, the first practical question was who the building control body was, the local authority or a private approved inspector, because it decided who we booked inspections with, what they wanted to see before work was covered up, and how long sign-off took at the end. The jobs that ran late were rarely the complicated ones; they were the ones where nobody asked that question until the first inspection was due.

## A short checklist before design starts

- Get the plot documents in hand: title deed, affection plan and any plot sale agreement or design guidelines from the developer.
- Ask your consultant, in writing, which authority covers the plot and on what basis.
- Get the master developer's current guidelines and NOC process for your community.
- Confirm the consultant's experience with that authority and the contractor's registration under it.
- Put developer review, the permit and staged inspections into the programme as separate lines, with an allowance for at least one round of comments.

If you are buying, do this before you commit. Our [plot checks guide](/insights/buying-a-villa-plot-in-dubai-checks/) covers the rest.
