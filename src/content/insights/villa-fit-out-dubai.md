A villa fit-out is everything that turns a structure into a finished home: walls, ceilings, floors, joinery, kitchens, bathrooms, lighting and the final fix of the services. In Dubai it applies to new shell-and-core villas, to developer villas that owners want to upgrade, and to older villas stripped back to the frame. The work itself is familiar; what makes fit-outs go wrong is the number of parties involved and the decisions that arrive late.

## What does a villa fit-out include?

| Package | Typical content | Who usually designs it |
|---|---|---|
| Internal layout | Partitions, doors, ceilings, bulkheads | Interior designer, with the consultant if walls are structural |
| Floors and walls | Screeds, stone, tiles, timber, wall finishes, paint | Interior designer |
| Joinery | Kitchens, wardrobes, vanities, panelling, doors | Interior designer and joinery maker |
| Wet areas | Bathrooms, waterproofing, sanitaryware | Interior designer, with the services designer |
| Services final fix | Air conditioning outlets and controls, lighting, switches, sockets, plumbing fittings | Services designer (MEP) |
| Systems | Smart home, AV, security, data | Specialist, coordinated with MEP |
| Loose furniture | Furniture, curtains, rugs, art | Interior designer, often outside the fit-out contract |

A clear fit-out contract states which of these are in and which are out. The most expensive gaps are usually between the fit-out contractor and the services installers, and between the contractor and a separate joinery maker.

## Who is involved, and where the gaps are

A typical villa fit-out has at least three parties: an interior designer, a fit-out contractor and the services (MEP) designers and installers. Add a joinery workshop, a stone supplier, a lighting designer and a smart-home specialist and the list grows quickly.

On the contractor's side, the costliest problems we saw were rarely about workmanship. They were about interfaces: the ceiling designed without room for the air-conditioning ducts, the joinery made to drawings that did not match the walls as built, the lighting scheme that arrived after the ceilings were closed. Each one is a variation, a delay, or both.

The cure is coordination before work starts: one set of drawings showing ceilings, services and joinery together, agreed by everyone who has to build them.

## Approvals for a fit-out

In a master community, the developer's consent usually comes first. Emaar Community Management says any alteration to the villa has to be approved by Emaar, and contractors need community entry permits even for work that needs no NOC ([Emaar Community Management](https://www.ecm.ae/en/communities/arabian-ranches/lifestyle/faqs/home-modifications-arabian-ranches/)). Nakheel says an NOC is needed before any alterations, and sets conditions for contractors including a security deposit and access permits ([Nakheel](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us)).

Work that changes structure, services or the outside of the house is more likely to need a building permit as well, from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority depending on where your plot sits. Your registered consultant handles that application. Do not let a fit-out contractor start before consent and access permits are in hand; the cost of a crew waiting at the gate lands on you.

## Shell-and-core villas

Some developers hand over villas as a shell and core, with the structure, envelope and main services in place and the interiors left to the owner. That gives freedom, but it also means:

- **The services were designed before your layout.** Check that air-conditioning capacity, drainage points and electrical supply suit the layout you want before the design is fixed.
- **The developer's handover condition matters.** Snag the shell before fit-out starts, or defects in the shell become arguments with the fit-out contractor later. Our [snagging guide](/insights/villa-snagging-checklist-dubai/) covers what to check.
- **Warranties can overlap.** Know which defects belong to the developer and which to the fit-out contractor.

## Budget: what moves the cost

There is no reliable single rate for a villa fit-out, because the specification swings the price enormously. As a reference point, Engel & Völkers puts interiors from about AED 200,000 for basic finishes to AED 800,000–1 million or more for luxury villas ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)). The biggest drivers are:

- **Joinery.** Bespoke kitchens, wardrobes and panelling are usually the largest line.
- **Stone.** Natural stone floors and bathrooms cost far more than porcelain, in material and in labour.
- **Ceilings and lighting.** Feature ceilings and layered lighting take time and coordination.
- **Systems.** Smart-home and AV packages can add a large sum and need early design.
- **Changes after the start.** The cheapest fit-out is the one whose design is finished before anyone starts on site.

## Turnkey or separate designer and contractor?

| Route | Advantages | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Turnkey design-and-fit-out company | One contract, one point of responsibility, often faster | The firm designs, prices and checks its own work; specification can drift to what suits the firm |
| Separate interior designer and fit-out contractor | Design independent of construction; competitive tender on a full design | More interfaces for the owner to manage; needs a complete design before tender |

Either route can work. What matters is a detailed priced scope, a fixed specification, payments against progress you have seen, and someone on your side checking the work before it is covered. Our guide to [design-and-build or a separate architect](/insights/design-build-vs-separate-architect-villa/) explains who checks whom under each route.

## Programme: order early

Long-lead items set the fit-out programme, not the site work. Bespoke joinery, natural stone, imported sanitaryware and specialist lighting can each take months. Decide them during design and order them as early as the contract allows.
