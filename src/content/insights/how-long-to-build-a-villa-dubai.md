A custom villa in Dubai typically takes 12–24 months from plot to keys, and 24–36 months for a custom luxury villa, according to Real Estate Club Dubai. Construction itself is usually 8–14 months; the rest goes on design, approvals, choosing a contractor and handover. On most projects the months that are lost are lost on paper, before and during the build, not in the concrete and blockwork.

That last point is the one owners rarely hear. What follows is where the time actually goes, which steps you can shorten and which you cannot.

## How long does it take to build a villa in Dubai?

Here is the full journey, with published durations where they exist and our own planning allowances where they do not.

| Phase | Typical duration | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Plot checks, brief and budget | 1–3 months | Our planning allowance |
| Concept and detailed design | 3–4 months | Real Estate Club Dubai |
| Master-developer and authority approvals | 2–3 months | Real Estate Club Dubai |
| Bidding and contractor appointment | 1–2 months | Our planning allowance |
| Construction | 8–14 months | Real Estate Club Dubai |
| Completion, snagging and handover | 1–2 months | Real Estate Club Dubai |
| Sum of phases above, end to end | 16–28 months | Our arithmetic |
| **Plot to keys, published headline (phases overlap)** | **12–24 months** (24–36 for custom luxury) | Real Estate Club Dubai |

The published 12–24 months is shorter than the phases added end to end because it assumes some of them overlap. Two things stand out. First, roughly a third to a half of the elapsed time passes before anyone breaks ground. Second, the phases are not fixed blocks. A well-run project overlaps some of them, for example by preparing the bid package while approvals are in progress. A poorly run one stretches each of them, usually because a decision is waiting on someone.

For the step-by-step sequence and who does what at each stage, see our guide on [how to build a villa in Dubai](/insights/how-to-build-a-villa-in-dubai/).

## Where do the months actually go?

On paper, a villa programme looks like a construction programme with some design at the front. In reality, the critical path runs through information for most of the project.

**Design.** Three to four months is realistic for a resolved design across architecture, structure and MEP. It is not realistic if the brief is still moving. Every change to room sizes, a basement or the façade sends the design back a step, and each step means redrawing, recoordinating and, later, repricing.

**Approvals.** The permit comes from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, and the service-authority NOCs are now gathered through one online window. In a master-planned community, the master developer's own design review and NOC usually comes first, and that review can take longer than the permit.

**Bidding.** Inviting bids, answering bidder questions, comparing returns like-for-like and negotiating the contract takes weeks, not days. Rushing it is a false economy, because the months you save here are often lost in variations and disputes later.

**Construction.** This is where owners expect the time to go, and where it is easiest to see. It is also where earlier slippage surfaces, because late design information shows up as stoppages on site.

**Handover.** The building can be physically complete weeks before you can live in it. Dubai Municipality's residential procedure describes a joint completion inspection, the completion certificate and then service connection. Snagging runs in parallel.

From the contractor's side, we could usually tell within the first two months whether a job would finish on time. It was rarely the ground or the weather that told us. It was how quickly the client and the design team answered questions.

## How long does a villa building permit take in Dubai?

Once the drawings are right, the permit itself is not usually the slow part. Khaleej Times reported in August 2026 that Dubai Municipality is developing an AI system intended to cut villa permit approvals from days to minutes, rolled out in phases through Build in Dubai. The months go on preparing a compliant submission, master-developer review and rounds of comments; our [guide to the Dubai villa building permit](/insights/dubai-municipality-villa-building-permit/) sets out each step and where programmes slip. Abu Dhabi has moved the same way with its Binaa platform; see our [Abu Dhabi villa permit guide](/insights/abu-dhabi-villa-permit-tamm/).

## How long does villa construction take, stage by stage?

Published guidance puts construction at 8–14 months. The table below is our own rule of thumb for how that time is typically spread on a two-storey villa without a basement. Stages overlap, so the durations do not simply add up.

| Construction stage | Indicative duration | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Mobilisation and enabling works | 2–4 weeks | Site set-up, access, temporary services |
| Excavation and substructure | 1–2 months | Ground conditions, water table, any basement |
| Structural frame and slabs | 2–3 months | Number of floors, spans, authority inspections |
| Envelope: blockwork, roof, waterproofing, glazing | 2–3 months | Glazing lead times, façade complexity |
| MEP first fix and internal walls | 2–3 months | Cooling strategy, automation, coordination |
| Finishes and second fix | 2–4 months | Stone, joinery, owner decisions |
| External works, pool and landscaping | 1–3 months, overlapping | Levels, drainage, planting season |
| Testing, commissioning and completion inspections | 3–6 weeks | Systems complexity, authority inspections |

Add a basement and the substructure stage can easily double. Dubai Municipality's procedure refers to staged inspections, such as shoring, foundations and slabs, so each structural milestone also carries a short wait for inspection, which a good programme allows for.

## Does the summer slow construction in Dubai?

It changes the working day. The UAE midday break, run by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation, bans outdoor work under direct sunlight from 12:30pm to 3pm every day between 15 June and 15 September. 2026 was its 22nd consecutive year. Exemptions include concrete pours and asphalt laying where the work cannot be interrupted (Khaleej Times).

Contractors adapt with early starts, shaded work areas and by shifting internal trades into the hottest months. Even so, three months of shortened outdoor days, combined with heat and humidity, reduce output on structural and external work. In our view, a programme that shows the same weekly progress in August as in January has not been thought through. Ask the contractor how the summer is reflected in its programme, and which activities it plans to do then.

## What makes a villa build take longer?

In our experience, these are the usual culprits, roughly in order of how often we saw them:

1. **Late owner decisions.** Tiles, sanitaryware, joinery and automation chosen after the programme needs them. The delay is the owner's, and so is the cost.
2. **Design changes after the permit.** Moving walls or adding floor area can mean resubmission and lost months.
3. **Long-lead items ordered late.** Large-format or structural glazing, imported stone, bespoke joinery, lifts and specialist cooling equipment can take months to arrive. If they are not ordered early, the programme waits for them.
4. **Basements and difficult ground.** Waterfront and reclaimed plots in particular can need shoring and dewatering that add weeks before the frame begins.
5. **Slow answers to site questions.** Each request for information from the contractor that waits a fortnight is a fortnight someone can claim.
6. **Contractor resources and cash flow.** A contractor stretched across too many sites, or short of cash, slows down quietly. A valuation running ahead of physical progress is an early warning.
7. **The end of the job.** Commissioning, completion inspections, connections and snagging are often under-planned, and the last 5% of the work takes a disproportionate share of the time.

On the contractor's side, we always planned the programme so that the client's decisions sat on the critical path. That is not sharp practice; it is how programmes are drawn. It does mean that any late owner decision becomes immediate, claimable delay. We explain how float works in [what a builder knows that most villa owners don't](/insights/what-the-contractor-knows/).

## Why does delay cost so much?

Because many of the contractor's site costs are time-related. Turner & Townsend put contractor preliminaries in Dubai at 12–14% of the contract (as cited by PRASOON). Those costs are priced per week or per month, so every extra month on site carries its own cost before any additional work is done. Add the owner's own costs, such as rent elsewhere, finance and currency exposure, and a three-month overrun becomes a significant line in the budget. ## How can you shorten the time to build a villa?

You cannot compress concrete curing or authority inspections. You can remove most of the waiting around them.

- **Fix the brief before design starts.** The cheapest month to change your mind is the first one.
- **Finish the design before pricing.** A complete design gives real bids, fewer provisional sums and fewer stoppages on site.
- **Overlap where it is safe.** Prepare the bid package while approvals run, rather than after.
- **Order long-lead items early.** Identify them during design and agree when each must be ordered.
- **Agree a decision schedule.** List every owner decision with the latest date it can be made, and have the contractor confirm those dates against its programme.
- **Test the programme at bid stage.** An unusually short programme with low preliminaries is often a claim waiting to happen.
- **Review progress monthly against the programme, not just the payments.** Small slippage is easy to recover in month three and very hard in month eleven.
- **Plan the end early.** Commissioning, inspections, connections and [snagging](/insights/villa-snagging-checklist-dubai/) should be on the programme from the start.
