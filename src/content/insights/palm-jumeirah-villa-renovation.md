Renovating a villa on Palm Jumeirah starts with a Nakheel NOC, then a building permit from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits. Anything that changes the structure, the footprint, the façade or the pool needs both; purely cosmetic interior work usually needs far less. The money is usually lost not in the approvals but in what the contractor finds once the walls, roofs and pool shells are opened up.

## Who approves a villa renovation on Palm Jumeirah?

Palm Jumeirah is a Nakheel master community. That means two layers of approval sit above your design.

**The master developer.** Renovation and extension approvals in Nakheel communities start with an NOC application through Nakheel's portal. Nakheel is checking that your proposal respects the community's design guidelines, plot limits and frontage rules, and that the works will be carried out in a way that does not disrupt neighbours or damage shared infrastructure. Expect to submit drawings, contractor details and, for larger work, a deposit or undertaking. A Nakheel design approval issued in June 2026 shows the pattern: the design is checked against the plot's development control regulations, the approval is valid for six months, and a Nakheel site access NOC and a security deposit for modification works are required before any construction starts, applied for through Nakheel's online services ([Nakheel](https://www.cgidubai.gov.in/content/Nakheel-PD-approval-PD_IC1-CTYL-16_013_09-Jun-2026.pdf)). The same document notes two Palm-specific points: PVC site hoarding is mandatory on Palm Jumeirah, and the gas NOC there comes from a different provider from other Nakheel communities.

**The permitting authority.** With the NOC in hand, your registered consultant applies for the building permit from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits. Service-authority NOCs now come through one online window rather than door to door. The Build in Dubai platform now covers alterations as well as new builds. Our [step-by-step guide to Dubai villa building permits](/insights/dubai-municipality-villa-building-permit/) explains what the consultant submits and what the owner signs.

The owner appoints the consultant and the contractor. The consultant prepares and submits the drawings; the contractor carries out the work under the permit. Our role at Eastbridge is to represent you alongside both, not to replace either.

## What needs approval, and what usually does not?

Requirements change, and Nakheel and the authorities publish their own lists, so treat the following as a guide to the conversation, not a ruling.

| Type of work | Typical approval route |
|---|---|
| Extensions, new rooms, enclosing terraces, basements | Nakheel NOC plus building permit; full drawings and structural design |
| Façade changes: new openings, cladding, glazing, colour | Nakheel NOC (design guidelines apply); permit where structure or openings change |
| New or rebuilt swimming pool | Nakheel NOC plus permit; structural and MEP drawings |
| Structural alterations: removing walls, new beams, new stairs | Nakheel NOC plus permit; structural engineer's design |
| MEP replacement: new AC system, rewiring, plumbing | Often requires NOC and service authority approvals, depending on scope |
| Kitchen and bathroom refits with no layout or services change | Usually lighter or no approval, but confirm with Nakheel first |
| Painting, flooring, joinery, loose furniture | Generally cosmetic; access and contractor registration rules still apply |

The point we make to every owner is simple. If you are not sure whether something needs approval, assume it does until the consultant has confirmed otherwise in writing. Unapproved work on the Palm tends to surface at the worst possible moment: when you sell, when you apply for a later extension, or when a neighbour complains. Nakheel has enforced the point before: in 2018, Gulf News reported that Jumeirah Islands owners who altered villas without approval faced fines, alongside a charge for the added space ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/uae/jumeirah-islands-owners-prevented-from-modifying-villas-without-approval-1.1172089)).

## In what order should a Palm Jumeirah renovation run?

A clean sequence saves months. The one we work to looks like this.

1. **Brief and budget.** Agree what you want, what you will spend, and what you would cut first if the numbers come in high.
2. **Condition survey and opening-up.** Before any design is fixed, get the existing villa properly inspected: roofs, basement, wet areas, pool, MEP plant and distribution. Where it matters, open up small areas.
3. **Concept design and early cost check.** Your consultant develops the design; get it priced at concept stage, not after the drawings are finished.
4. **Nakheel NOC.** Submit through the Nakheel portal. Our guide to the [Nakheel NOC for villa renovations](/insights/nakheel-noc-villa-renovation/) explains what triggers one and how to sequence the contractor around it.
5. **Detailed design and building permit.** Structural, MEP and architectural drawings, then the permit application through the unified window.
6. **Contractor selection.** Compare bids like-for-like on the same drawings and specification. Our guide on [how to choose a villa contractor in Dubai](/insights/how-to-choose-a-villa-contractor-dubai/) covers the questions to ask.
7. **Contract.** Agree scope, price basis, programme, payment terms and how changes are handled. See [villa construction contracts in the UAE](/insights/villa-construction-contracts-uae/).
8. **Works, inspections and completion.** Staged authority inspections, then completion and handover.
9. **Snagging and defects period.** Walk the villa properly before you accept it.

The most expensive mistake is skipping step 2. On the contractor's side, an incomplete survey was where the commercial opportunity sat. A builder pricing a refurbishment against thin information knows that whatever is hidden will come back as a variation, at rates you no longer have any leverage to negotiate.

## What hidden problems do older Palm villas have?

Many of the original frond villas are now around two decades old. They were built quickly, at scale, in a demanding marine environment. In our experience the same issues come up again and again on coastal houses of that age, wherever they are.

### Waterproofing

Roof membranes, wet-room tanking, terraces over habitable rooms and basement waterproofing all have a finite life. Failures often show as staining, blistering paint or musty rooms long after the damage has started. Replacing a roof or basement membrane is not a cosmetic job; it means stripping finishes, possibly lifting screeds, and doing it before any new interior goes in.

### MEP services

Air-conditioning systems, ductwork, pipework and electrical distribution installed when the villa was built may be near the end of their life or undersized for how you want to live now. If you are replacing finishes, it is almost always cheaper to replace tired services at the same time than to open the villa up again in three years.

### Salt exposure

Wind-borne salt attacks external metalwork, fixings, balustrades, window frames, AC condensers and, in the worst cases, reinforcement in concrete where the cover has been compromised. Cracking and rust staining on external concrete need investigating by a structural engineer, not painting over.

### The pool

Pools on older villas commonly have leaking shells, failed tiling and outdated plant. A pool is a structure, and a rebuild sits firmly in the approvals column. One Dubai engineering consultancy puts a basic fibreglass pool at AED 80,000–120,000 and a standard concrete villa pool at AED 150,000–250,000 ([Optimal Engineering](https://www.optimal.ae/blog/cost-to-build-a-villa-in-dubai)), which is a useful reality check if the pool is on your list.

On the contractor's side, the first thing we priced on any refurbishment of an older coastal house was the risk in the envelope. Interiors are predictable. Roofs, basements and anything facing the sea are not, and an honest contractor will tell you so.

The contractor's guide cites no sources for its figures. Property Finder's table ties its bands to villa type and scope; it names communities only as examples, not as a reason for a price difference. A full refurbishment of a Palm villa, with new services, waterproofing, kitchen, bathrooms, joinery and external works, will usually sit well inside the luxury band. Three further allowances are worth carrying from the start:

- **Contingency.** Engel & Völkers says a contingency of 10–15% of total project cost is widely recommended on new builds ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)). For a renovation of an older villa, in our experience you should sit at the top of that range or above until the opening-up survey is done.
- **Preliminaries.** Turner & Townsend's 2025 UAE market intelligence puts preliminaries in Dubai at 12% of construction cost on a 2,500 m² project ([Turner & Townsend](https://marketintelligence.turnerandtownsend.com/uaemi-2025/construction-cost-performance/)). On a renovation in an occupied community, site set-up, protection, access restrictions and waste removal are real costs, and they should be visible in the bid.
- **Inflation.** Currie & Brown forecast UAE cost escalation of around 3% for 2026 ([TradeArabia](https://tradearabia.com/News/389130/Construction-costs-set-to-rise-this-year-in-Saudi-Arabia-and-UAE-report)); Turner & Townsend forecast 4% for Dubai. On a long design and approvals period, that matters.

## What are the budget traps on a Palm renovation?

The traps are rarely dramatic. They are small decisions that compound.

- **Provisional sums for the unknown.** If the bid carries a modest provisional sum for "waterproofing repairs" or "MEP remedials", the real cost is still unknown. Push for investigation before signing, or at least a clear rate.
- **Specification drift.** Upgrading a finish here and a fitting there feels small. Across a whole villa it is not. Agree a specification and track every change against it.
- **Design changes after the NOC.** Altering an approved design can mean resubmitting to Nakheel and the authority, with time and fees attached.
- **Programme slippage.** Every extra month is another month of preliminaries, another month of rent if you have moved out, and another month before you can use the house.
- **Exclusions.** Landscaping, pool plant, AV, lighting control and loose furniture are frequently outside the main contract. Ask what is not included, not just what is.

## Renovating in an occupied community

Palm Jumeirah is a lived-in, high-value neighbourhood, and your contractor will be working next to people who did not choose to have a building site beside them. Expect access hours, restrictions on deliveries and parking, noise limits, requirements for hoarding and site tidiness, and rules on where waste and materials can sit. Narrow frond roads and shared access make logistics harder than on an open plot.

Two practical points. First, a contractor who has not priced for these constraints will either cut corners or come back for more money. Second, good relations with neighbours are worth protecting; a polite letter before work starts and a contact number for complaints prevent a great deal of friction.

If you plan to stay in the villa during the works, be realistic. For anything involving services, waterproofing or structure, living in it lengthens the programme and adds cost, because the contractor has to phase work around you and protect occupied areas.

## Should I renovate or rebuild my Palm villa?

This is the question owners least want to ask, and the one that most often needs asking.

Renovation makes sense when the structure is sound, the layout broadly works, and your brief is mainly about finishes, kitchens, bathrooms, services and outdoor space. Rebuild deserves serious consideration when the brief keeps expanding towards new structure, a reworked envelope, full services replacement and significant extensions. At that point you can end up paying for much of a new house while still working within the constraints of the old one, and carrying the risk of whatever the existing structure is hiding.

Our rule of thumb: once the renovation scope starts to look like a new building, get both routes priced properly by your consultant before committing. A rebuild means a longer approvals route and a longer programme, but it also gives you a new house with known construction and a full set of warranties.
