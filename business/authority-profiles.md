## 6. Dubai Chamber of Commerce (membership-based)

**What it is.** Dubai Chambers membership is **paid and membership-based**, open to "mainland and free zone companies engaged in commercial, industrial, or professional activities" (https://dubaichambers.com/new-membership, checked 7 November 2026).

**Cost as published.** Annual fees range from AED 300 to AED 2,200 depending on legal form and activity; **free zone companies pay AED 2,200**. Members of the Mohammed Bin Rashid Establishment for SMEs get a five-year exemption. The page says total service fees may vary.

**Is it required?** Meydan Free Zone says Chamber membership is "optional but recommended if you are involved in trade, export, or government contracts", and that Meydan licensees are not registered automatically (https://www.meydanfz.ae/blog/how-to-register-your-business-with-dubai-chamber-of-commmerce).

**Directory.** The membership page lists benefits (smart services, events and networks, awards, mediation, business facilitation) but does **not** say members get a public directory listing. A "Commercial Directory" link exists under its Knowledge Centre; whether members appear in it is unconfirmed. **Ask before paying.**

**Recommendation.** Optional. Worth AED 2,200 only if Phil wants the networking events or a certificate of origin route; not as a backlink. If yes:
1. Go to https://dubaichambers.com/new-membership → **Apply** → create an account on the Dubai Chambers portal.
2. Documents for a free zone company: valid trade licence copy; passport or Emirates ID copy of the manager; attested memorandum of association, share certificate or certificate of formation; and a manager's letter of authorisation if an attorney applies.
3. In the application notes, ask: "Are members listed in the Dubai Chambers Commercial Directory, and can the listing include our website?"
4. Pay, download the digital certificate (two to five working days per Meydan FZ). Company name and address exactly as section 0.

- **Section:** Opinion / expert comment (ITP describes Construction Week Middle East's output as including "expert opinion", https://www.itp.com/brands/construction-week-middle-east/).
- **Contact route:** the February 2026 issue masthead lists **Julianne Tolentino, Editor**, and **Thomas Mackie, Group Editor**, each with an itp.com address and a Dubai phone number (https://mags.itp.com/ConstructionWeek/2026/0832_CW-ME-FEBRUARY2026/). **Pitch (150 words)**
```
Dear Julianne,

Villa budgets in Dubai rarely blow up in one go. They leak, a line at a time: provisional sums that were never real, preliminaries that grow with every week of delay, variations agreed on site without a price, and payment applications that run ahead of the work.

I would like to offer Construction Week an opinion piece of about 800 words on where that money goes and how private owners can see it earlier. For 21 years I ran P Chapman Construction, a premium residential builder in the UK, so I priced and built these jobs from the contractor's side. I now lead Eastbridge Strategic Partners, an independent client-side project manager for villas in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

The piece is practical, not promotional, and uses published figures only (Turner & Townsend on preliminaries and cost inflation). I can deliver within a week of your go-ahead.

Kind regards,
Philip Chapman
+971 56 744 0057
```

**Outline**
- Where the leaks start: incomplete design, provisional sums and allowances that were never priced, and what Turner & Townsend's preliminaries figures (Dubai 12% on a 2,500 m² project) mean when a programme slips.
- During the build: variations agreed on site without a price or time effect, and payment applications valued ahead of progress; what an owner should ask for each month.
- The fix is process, not suspicion: a frozen brief, a priced change log, a monthly cost report against budget and a 10 to 15% contingency that is actually held.
