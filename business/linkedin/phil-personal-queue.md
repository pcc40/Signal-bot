### 13 Nov — stalled build
Your villa build has gone quiet. Fewer men on site, calls not returned. What do you do in the first ten days?

As the builder, I saw how stalls start. What the owner does early usually decides how they end:

• Photograph and video the whole site, dated, before anything moves
• Write to the contractor asking for the reason and a return date
• Pause further payments until you've reconciled what's been paid against what's been built
• Read the suspension and termination clauses with a lawyer before you act

A wrongful termination can turn a strong position into a weak one. And in Dubai, a replacement contractor can't start until a new building permit is issued in its name.

### 14 Nov — how long
How long does it really take to build a villa in Dubai? The published answer is 12–24 months from plot to keys, and 24–36 for a custom luxury villa.

The construction itself is often the shorter part. Before a contractor even mobilises, you've usually been through:

• Plot checks, brief and budget
• Concept and detailed design
• Master-developer and authority approvals
• Bidding and appointing the contractor

What slows a build most is rarely the site. It's late decisions, approvals running behind the programme, and long-lead items ordered too late.

### 15 Nov — plot checks
Buying a villa plot in Dubai? The price is the easiest part to check.

What decides what you can actually build sits elsewhere:

• The affection plan: area, boundaries and permitted use
• The master developer's design guidelines: setbacks, height, coverage and style
• Build-by deadlines, and what happens if you miss them
• Ground conditions, dewatering and utility connections

Then run a realistic build budget alongside the plot price before you commit, not after.

### 16 Nov — renovation mistakes
As the contractor, I saw the same villa renovation mistakes again and again. Almost none of them were about workmanship.

• Starting on site before the scope is frozen
• Pricing the villa without opening it up first
• Choosing finishes after the strip-out
• Mobilising before the developer NOC and permit are in place
• Comparing quotes on the bottom line
• Paying ahead of the work

Each one turns into variations, idle site days or lost leverage. Every one is avoidable before the first wall comes down.

### 20 Nov — defects liability period
You've got the keys. Your contractor's obligations haven't ended, and neither have yours.

The defects liability period, often twelve months on building works, is your window to find and report defects while the retention is still held. Use it:

• Test every wet area in daily use in the first month
• Run every AC unit for a full day in July and August
• Check roofs, terraces and drains after the first heavy rain
• Re-inspect the whole villa in month 10 or 11, before the retention is released

Beyond that, UAE law makes the contractor and engineer jointly liable for ten years for defects that threaten the building's stability or safety.

### 21 Nov — kitchen and bathroom costs
What does a kitchen renovation cost in a Dubai villa? Depending on which published source you read, a complete villa kitchen is anywhere from AED 15,000 to AED 150,000.

The sources aren't wrong. They're pricing different things. What really moves the number:

• Whether the layout changes, and the plumbing and electrics move with it
• Custom joinery or off-the-shelf cabinets
• The stone and the appliances
• Developer approvals and community access permits

Fix the scope before you compare quotes. Then the numbers mean something.

### 22 Nov — choosing a contractor
The lowest villa bid is rarely the cheapest build. For 21 years I was the one pricing them.

Before you compare totals, compare the same things:

• Preliminaries, and what share of the total they are
• Provisional sums, and whether they're realistic
• Exclusions, priced back in
• Programme, payment terms, bonds and retention

Then ask who will actually run your job, and go and see their last villas.

It works both ways. Good contractors price sharper for a client with complete drawings, a fair contract and someone on their side who understands construction.

### 23 Nov — owner's rep vs PM vs consultant
Registered consultant, project manager, owner's representative. On a Dubai villa they can sound like the same job. They aren't.

• Your registered consultant designs the villa, makes the authority submissions and has the statutory role on site
• A project manager serves whoever appoints them, and that isn't always you
• An owner's representative works for the owner only, with no stake in the design or the build

One question sorts them quickly: who else pays you on this project? For an owner's rep, the only acceptable answer is nobody.
