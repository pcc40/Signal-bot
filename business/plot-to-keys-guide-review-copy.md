## In short

Building a villa in Dubai or Abu Dhabi takes an owner through 16 stages and the 131 decisions listed below, from checking the plot to the end of the defects period. Your registered consultant designs and deals with the authority; your contractor builds. Every decision about money, time and what you accept is yours. Eastbridge sits beside you for each one.

## Key points

- An owner building a villa in Dubai or Abu Dhabi goes through 16 stages and makes the 131 decisions listed in this guide, from checking the plot to releasing the last of the retention.
- Statutory design, authority submissions and statutory site inspections stay with your registered consultant; building, site safety and the works insurance stay with your contractor; every decision on money, time and acceptance stays with you.
- The decisions that fix most of the cost come before construction: what the plot allows, the brief, the budget, the design freeze and the contract.
- Rules vary by plot: the permit authority is Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, and each master developer has its own guidelines, deadlines and deposits.

## Stage 1 of 16: Before you buy the plot

*Typical duration: 1 to 3 weeks (our planning allowance)*

Everyone quotes the plot price. What the rules let you build, by when, and at what cost decides whether it is a good purchase.

### What you have to decide

1. Whether the house you want fits the permitted area, coverage, height and floors.
2. Whether you can live with the setbacks and the rules on style and materials.
3. Whether you can meet any build-by deadline in the sale terms.
4. Whether the service charges are acceptable every year, built or not.
5. Whether to pay for a soil investigation and level survey before you commit.
6. Whether access, orientation, neighbours and views suit the house.
7. Whether the whole budget works: plot, fees, build, VAT, contingency, landscape and furniture.
8. Whether to buy, negotiate or walk away.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

A plot sold "with approved drawings" may come with nothing you can use. One Nakheel preliminary design approval, published in June 2026, was valid for six months at most unless extended in writing, and stayed subject to the plot's sale agreement ([Nakheel](https://www.cgidubai.gov.in/content/Nakheel-PD-approval-PD_IC1-CTYL-16_013_09-Jun-2026.pdf)). Build-by deadlines and penalties vary by developer and by sale. We found no published standard, so read yours.

### Who is responsible

- **The master developer** sets the plot's development controls, design guidelines and any deadline to build.
- **Your lawyer** checks title and the terms you are asked to sign.

- We read the design guidelines and the sale terms, and tell you what they allow and commit you to.
- We look at the plot as a builder would: access, levels, neighbours and buildability.
- We price the whole project, in a written report within five working days of receiving the plot documents.

**You receive:** Written plot review report; Design guideline summary; Build budget range; Questions for the seller or developer.

## Stage 2 of 16: Buying the plot

*Typical duration: 2 to 6 weeks (our planning allowance)*

The purchase is a legal transaction with construction consequences. What you sign sets the rules for what you build.

### What you have to decide

1. Which UAE-qualified lawyer reads the sale agreement before you sign.
2. Whose name the plot goes in: yours, joint names or a company.
3. Whether to accept the building obligations, resale restrictions and penalties as written.
4. How you pay: cash, the developer's payment plan or finance.
5. Who bears the transfer fees, which the agreement decides.
6. Whether any design approvals the seller holds can pass to you.
7. What you must hold at handover: title deed, plot plan, development controls and cleared charges.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

Dubai Land Department lists the sale registration fee as 2% of the sale value for the seller and 2% for the buyer, plus a service partner fee of AED 4,000 and VAT ([Dubai Land Department](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-sale-registration)). The two 2% shares come to AED 400,000 on a plot of AED 10 million. Who actually pays is whatever your agreement says.

### Who is responsible

- **Your lawyer** advises on the agreement and the transfer. We do not give legal advice.
- **Dubai Land Department** registers the sale and issues the title deed. Abu Dhabi has its own registration process.

### What Eastbridge does at this stage

- We read the sale terms as a builder: build-by dates, penalties and design obligations, flagged for your lawyer.
- We check the plot documents against what a consultant will ask for on day one.
- We update the total budget with the real purchase and transfer costs.

**You receive:** Sale terms notes; Questions for the seller or developer.

## Stage 3 of 16: Brief and budget

*Typical duration: 2 to 6 weeks (our planning allowance)*

The brief is the cheapest document to change and the one that fixes most of the cost. Nobody else is paid to write it for you.

### What you have to decide

1. The rooms: bedrooms, living rooms, majlis, staff rooms, study, gym, garages.
2. The size, and whether the plot and the money both allow it.
3. Basement or no basement.
4. The quality level, which sets the rate per square foot.
5. What the budget covers beyond construction: fees, VAT, pool, landscape, kitchens, furniture.
6. How much contingency to hold, and who holds it.
7. How the build is funded, and what cash is needed each month.
8. The date you want to move in, and how fixed it is.
9. What you will not compromise on, and what goes first if money is tight.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

A budget built on a rate per square foot is short before the first drawing. Published guides put design and engineering at 6 to 12% of the contract value ([Optimal Engineering](https://www.optimal.ae/blog/cost-to-build-a-villa-in-dubai)) and contingency at 10 to 15% ([Engel & Völkers](https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/construction-cost-dubai)); VAT is 5% ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-arab-emirates/corporate/other-taxes)). UAE nationals building their own home can reclaim that VAT; other owners cannot ([Federal Tax Authority](https://tax.gov.ae/en/services/vat.refund.for.uae.nationals.homebuilders.aspx)).

### Who is responsible

- **You** own the brief and the budget. No licensed party is responsible for either unless you appoint one.
- **Your registered consultant** designs to the brief, so the brief should exist first.

- We write the brief with you: rooms, sizes, finishes and priorities.
- We build the budget from the brief and the plot, with the contingency held by you, not the contractor.
- We set a programme showing the decisions you need to make and when, and forecast what falls due each month.

**You receive:** Brief, budget and programme; Payment and cash-flow forecast.

## Stage 4 of 16: Choosing the architect and registered consultant

*Typical duration: 3 to 6 weeks (our planning allowance)*

In Dubai only a registered engineering consultancy may design your villa and deal with the authority. Which firm, and on what terms, is your choice.

### What you have to decide

1. A separate consultant and contractor, or one design-and-build firm.
2. Which firms to approach, and whether each is registered for villa work.
3. One firm for everything, or a lead architect with a local registered consultant.
4. The scope: interiors, landscape, pool, lighting and statutory site inspection, or only some.
5. The fee basis: a percentage of build cost, a lump sum or a fee per stage.
6. The appointment terms: payments, insurance, termination and who owns the design.
7. Who the named designer is, and how much of their time you are buying.
8. Whether the interior designer and landscape architect are appointed now or later.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

In August 2025 Dubai Municipality warned consultancy offices for exaggerated structural designs on citizens' villas that caused unjustified increases in construction cost ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/warnings-issued-to-consultancy-offices-for-inflating-villa-designs/)). A fee set as a percentage of build cost does nothing to discourage that. And the cheapest fee proposal is often the one with the most left out.

### Who is responsible

- **Your registered consultant** is a firm authorised to practise engineering consultancy in Dubai; nobody may do so without authorisation ([Dubai Law No. 14 of 2025](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/october/05-10/mohammed-bin-rashid)). Statutory design, authority submissions and statutory site inspections are theirs.
- **You** appoint and pay the consultant directly.

- We help you shortlist firms, and check licences and registrations through the official registries.
- We compare fee proposals like-for-like, so nothing is missing from the scope you pay for.
- We read the appointment and give you the points to raise before you sign.

**You receive:** Shortlist with reasons; Licence and registration check record; Contract terms notes.

## Stage 5 of 16: Design: concept, developed and detailed

*Typical duration: 3 to 6 months (our planning allowance)*

Design is the cheapest place to change your mind and the most expensive place to lose control of cost.

### What you have to decide

1. The concept: layout, massing and where the house sits on the plot.
2. The structure: spans, cantilevers and basement, each justified against the code.
3. The glazing: sizes, sliding systems and solar control.
4. The air conditioning: the system, the plant location and the noise.
5. The interiors: kitchens, bathrooms, joinery and lighting, designed now or later.
6. The pool, the landscape and the external levels.
7. Home automation, audio-visual and security.
8. Which products the specification names, and which it leaves as allowances.
9. What to cut when a cost check comes in over budget, and what never to cut.
10. The design freeze: the date after which every change is a variation.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

Research into large Middle East projects found the two leading causes of trouble were changes to scope and design information that arrived late or incomplete ([RICS, reporting HKA](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/wbef/construction-disputes-in-the-middle-east-managing-the-worlds-highest-risk-of-overruns)). A villa is smaller; the mechanism is the same. As the builder in the UK, the costliest changes we saw were interior decisions made after the services were in the walls.

### Who is responsible

- **Your registered consultant** produces the design and is responsible for it meeting the building code.
- **You** approve each stage in writing. Later changes are measured against that signature.

### What Eastbridge does at this stage

- We check the design against the budget at concept, at detailed design and before pricing.
- Where structure or services cost looks high, we give you the questions to put to your consultant.
- We keep the decision log and keep your decisions on time.

**You receive:** Design-stage cost check notes; Value review notes; Decision log.

## Stage 6 of 16: Surveys and approvals

*Typical duration: 4 to 8 weeks for a straightforward project (Optimal Engineering)*

Two approvals, in order: the master developer's, then the authority's. Your consultant applies. Your part is the surveys, the signatures and the fees.

### What you have to decide

1. When to commission the soil investigation and the topographic survey.
2. Whether to seek the developer's concept approval before the design is finished.
3. How to answer the developer's comments: redesign, or make the case.
4. When the design is complete enough to go to the authority.
5. Whether to apply for early works, such as excavation, ahead of the main permit.
6. Paying the developer, authority and utility fees on the day they are due.
7. Whether a change after approval is worth a resubmission.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

Approvals expire. One property portal's guide says a Dubai Municipality permit should be issued within nine months of the drawings being approved, unless an extension is requested, and is then valid for two years ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/how-to-get-a-building-permit-dubai/)); we could not confirm this from the authority itself, so ask your consultant. The soil report comes before the foundations are designed; in Dubai Development Authority areas a single villa needs at least two boreholes ([DDA](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/DD544423AE174606AC17B53168436FF9.pdf)). In Abu Dhabi, a one-pearl Estidama rating is essential for a private project's building permit ([Abu Dhabi City Municipality](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/en/adm/Media-Centre/News/08Jan2025)).

### Who is responsible

- **Your registered consultant** prepares and submits the applications ([Dubai Municipality](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2020/06/Residential-Buildings_Building-Permits-Procedures.pdf)).
- **The authority** is Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits; in Abu Dhabi, the municipality through TAMM.

### What Eastbridge does at this stage

- We track every application with your consultant: what has been sent, what is waiting, and on whom.
- We keep your signatures, documents and fees ahead of the dates they are needed.
- We sit beside you at meetings with the consultant and the developer.

**You receive:** Approvals tracker.

## Stage 7 of 16: Tender and choosing the contractor

*Typical duration: about 4 weeks for the bill of quantities, then 4 to 8 weeks to tender (our planning allowance)*

This stage starts when your consultant releases the final construction drawings. A bill of quantities is prepared from them and goes to the shortlisted contractors with the tender pack. Contractor bids rarely compare directly. On paper the cheapest wins. In the final account it often does not.

### What you have to decide

1. Who to invite, and whether each is licensed and classified for your villa.
2. Whether the drawings are complete enough to price.
3. Who prepares the bill of quantities from the final construction drawings: your consultant or an independent quantity surveyor you appoint.
4. Which quantity surveyor to appoint, if you appoint your own: best value, not the lowest fee.
5. Allowing about four weeks after the final construction drawings are released for the bill of quantities to be prepared and checked before it goes to the contractors.
6. Which items are left as provisional sums, and at what allowance.
7. How bidders' questions are answered, so that all price the same thing.
8. How to compare: on like-for-like totals, not the headline price.
9. Who to interview, and which past clients to call.
10. What to negotiate first: price, programme and the named site team.
11. Who to appoint.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

On the contractor's side, we learned that an item missing from the bill of quantities does not go away: it comes back as a variation, priced after the contract is signed, when there is no competition left to keep the price honest. Since January 2026, contractors in Dubai must be on a single register and may only take work within their classification. Fines go up to AED 100,000, and an owner who engages a non-compliant contractor can be investigated too ([Mayer Brown on Dubai Law No. 7 of 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/dubai-overhauls-construction-sector-with-new-law-to-regulate-contracting-activities-what-employers-need-to-know-part-2)). Existing contractors were given a year to comply, so check on the day you appoint.

### Who is responsible

- **Your registered consultant** releases the final construction drawings and normally puts together the tender pack with the specification.
- **A quantity surveyor** (your consultant's or one you appoint) measures the drawings and prepares the bill of quantities.
- **You** decide who is invited and who is appointed.

### What Eastbridge does at this stage

- If you want an independent quantity surveyor, we help you find one: we invite proposals, review them with you and recommend the one that offers the best value, not just the lowest fee.
- We prepare the appointment for you to sign, so the surveyor is appointed directly by you.
- If you wish, we then review the bill of quantities in full against the drawings and specification before it goes to the contractors, so gaps, provisional sums and quantities you would pay for later are caught at tender, not in the final account.
- We check each bidder's licence and registration through the official registries.
- We put every bid into one like-for-like table: exclusions, provisional sums, preliminaries and programme.
- We call past clients, sit beside you at the interviews and give you a written recommendation.

## Stage 8 of 16: The contract

*Typical duration: 2 to 4 weeks (our planning allowance)*

The contract decides who carries each risk. Most of it is negotiable until the day you sign, and very little afterwards.

### What you have to decide

1. The form: Dubai Municipality's guidance contract, a FIDIC form or the contractor's own.
2. The price: lump sum, remeasured or a blend.
3. The payments: monthly against work valued on site, or fixed stages.
4. The advance payment, and the bank guarantee that secures it.
5. The retention: how much, and when each part is released.
6. The performance bond: its amount, wording and expiry date.
7. The completion date and the rate of delay damages.
8. Who may instruct a variation, and that it is priced first.
9. What insurance the contractor carries, and for how long.
10. The defects period, termination and how disputes are resolved.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

An advance of 10 to 20% is described as industry practice ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/highlights-of-the-laws-of-the-united-arab-emirates-the-peoples-republic-of-china-and-the-common-law/)): AED 500,000 on a contract of AED 5 million, paid before a block is laid, and unsecured without a guarantee. Retention is commonly 10%, with half released at handover and half after the defects period ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/global-construction-bulletin/2025/september-2025/security-of-payment-uae)). And a UAE court can reduce pre-agreed delay damages; for contracts made from 1 June 2026, the new Civil Code sets out when ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/The-Impact-of-the-New-Civil-Transactions-Law-on-Construction-Contracts-in-the-United-Arab-Emirates-3-9-2026)).

### Who is responsible

- **Your lawyer** advises on the contract's legal effect. Take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer before you sign.
- **Your registered consultant** is usually named in the contract to value payments and issue instructions.

- We read the draft the way a contractor reads it: Philip signed building contracts as the contractor, in the UK, at P Chapman Construction for 21 years.
- We set out the payment, retention, bond, delay and variation terms, with the points to raise before you sign.
- We test the payment schedule against the programme, so payments do not run ahead of the work.

**You receive:** Contract terms notes; Payment and cash-flow forecast.

## Stage 9 of 16: Before work starts

*Typical duration: 2 to 4 weeks (our planning allowance)*

Between signing and the first day on site, the rules of the job are set. Every later argument is measured against them.

### What you have to decide

1. The start date, and whether permit, contract and guarantees are in place for it.
2. Whether to release the advance before the guarantee and insurance are in your hands.
3. The baseline programme, against which every delay is measured.
4. The named site team and the main subcontractors.
5. The site set-up: fence, access, working hours, and what the neighbours are told.
6. Who may instruct the contractor, and how questions and payments will run.
7. Your decision schedule: every choice still to make, with its last date.
8. Which long-lead items to order now.

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

In Dubai, before a new building's permit is issued, the contractor must give the authority an insurance policy covering the works and third parties until a year after the completion certificate ([Executive Council Resolution No. 28 of 2022](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2022/Executive%20Council%20Resolution%20No.%20(28)%20of%202022%20Concerning%20the%20Insurance%20Policy%20Required.html)). Nakheel also requires a security deposit, a site access permit and a site fence ([Nakheel](https://www.nakheel.com/en/contact-us)). A contractor who has not allowed for these starts late, or charges them as extras.

### Who is responsible

- **The contractor** holds the works insurance, pays the developer's deposit, sets up the site and is responsible for safety on it.
- **Your registered consultant** takes up the statutory site inspection role.

- We check the guarantee, the bond and the insurance before we recommend that you release the advance.

## Stage 10 of 16: Construction

*Typical duration: 12 to 18 months, typically (Optimal Engineering)*

The contractor builds and your consultant carries out the statutory inspections. Your decisions now arrive one after another, each with a date on it.

### What you have to decide

1. Each month: how much to pay against the contractor's payment application.
2. Each variation: accept, reject or negotiate before it is built.
3. Each sample and mock-up: stone, façade, joinery, paint and fittings.
4. Each question from site, answered by the date the programme needs.
5. Each claim for more time: granted, refused or granted in part.
6. Whether work that is not right is redone before it is covered over.
7. When to release contingency, and for what.
8. When to visit, and what to see in person.
9. What to order from abroad, when, and who imports it.
10. Whether the programme is still realistic, and what to do if not.

### The stages worth seeing before they are covered up

Setting out; foundations and below-ground waterproofing; each slab before the concrete is poured; roof and wet-room waterproofing under test; pipes, ducts and cables before walls and ceilings are closed; plaster; glazing; fittings; finishes. The authority's own inspections cover shoring and foundations, then the structure ([Dubai Municipality](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2020/06/Residential-Buildings_Building-Permits-Procedures.pdf)).

### What it costs you if it goes wrong

Delay is paid for monthly. Turner & Townsend puts a contractor's preliminaries, the cost of running the site, at about 12% on a small Dubai project ([Turner & Townsend](https://marketintelligence.turnerandtownsend.com/uaemi-2025/construction-cost-performance/)), and those costs continue every month a job overruns. Summer is slower too: outdoor work under direct sun stops from 12:30pm to 3pm between 15 June and 15 September ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-midday-break-starts-june-15-work-under-direct-sunlight-banned-during-peak-afternoon-hours?amp=1)).

- **The contractor** builds, runs the site, and is responsible for workmanship and safety.

## Questions

**How many decisions does an owner make when building a villa in Dubai?**  
This guide lists 131 across 16 stages, from checking the plot before purchase to the end of the defects period. Some are made once, such as basement or no basement. Others repeat: every monthly payment and every variation is a decision of its own, so the real number on a two-year build is higher.

**How long does it take from buying a plot to getting the keys?**  
Engel & Völkers says most villas take 12 to 24 months from approval to completion. Design, approvals and choosing a contractor come before that. For a one-off villa, plan on two years or more from plot to keys; see [how long it takes to build a villa in Dubai](https://eastbridgestrategicpartners.com/insights/how-long-to-build-a-villa-dubai/).

**Which decisions can I not hand to anyone else?**  
The brief, the budget, who you appoint, what you sign, each payment, each change and whether you accept the finished house. We bring each one to you with the facts and a recommendation; the decision stays yours.

**What stays with the registered consultant and the contractor?**  
Statutory design, authority submissions and statutory site inspections stay with your registered consultant. Building the villa, site safety, the works insurance and the quality of the work stay with your contractor. Eastbridge does not replace either: we work alongside them and represent you.

**Do the rules differ between Dubai and Abu Dhabi, and between communities?**  
Yes. In Dubai the building permit comes from Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits. In Abu Dhabi it comes from the municipality through TAMM. Each master developer adds its own design guidelines, approval, deadlines and deposits, so read the documents for your own plot. Our [guide to the authorities](https://eastbridgestrategicpartners.com/insights/which-dubai-authority-covers-my-villa/) explains why.

**I live abroad. How many of these decisions need me in Dubai?**  
Few. Some choices of stone, joinery and fittings are better made in person, and the final walk-through is worth attending. The rest can come to you with photographs, options and a recommendation, recorded in a decision log so that nothing is agreed without your sign-off. See [building in Dubai while living abroad](https://eastbridgestrategicpartners.com/services/overseas-owners/).

**Can I use Eastbridge for one stage only, and what does it cost?**  
Yes. A review answers one question, such as a plot before you buy or a contractor's quote before you sign, with a written report. We publish three ranges by level of input. A review, such as a plot review before you buy, is AED 9,500–15,000 per review. Part service is a monthly fee agreed in the written scope. Full service, from brief to handover, is typically 1.75–3% of build cost. Each fee is agreed in a written scope of works after a free 30-minute call.
