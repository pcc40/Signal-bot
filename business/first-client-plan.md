# Eastbridge: first-client acquisition plan

Prepared 31 October 2026 for Philip Chapman. Internal working document; not for publication.

**Goal:** one signed, paid engagement as soon as possible. The fastest realistic route is a fixed-fee review (plot, contract, budget or stalled project) that turns into a full owner's representation appointment.

**Ground rules for every conversation, post and email in this plan**

- We represent the owner, alongside the owner's DM-registered consultant and the contractor. We never say we supervise, are the engineer, architect, contractor or consultant of record, obtain or submit permits, certify, prepare tender documents or prequalify contractors. We help the owner compare bids like for like and choose; the owner appoints.
- We never claim or imply UAE projects or existing Eastbridge clients. The Island E case study is illustrative and is always described that way. No client names, invented statistics, testimonials or "best" claims.
- We are paid only by the client. That cuts both ways. We take no referral fee, and we offer none: no paid introductions to brokers, architects or advisers. What we offer partners is a reliable, independent pair of hands for their clients, and introductions back to them where that is genuinely in the client's interest. This matches the [independence pledge](/independence/) and avoids putting an architect or broker in a conflict with their own client.
- Legal points ("take advice from a UAE-qualified lawyer") are referred to lawyers. We do not give legal advice.
- Contact businesses only through their published business channels. No scraping or buying of private individuals' data. For email outreach, follow the UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) and, for UK recipients, UK GDPR and PECR. One personal, relevant message to a published business address, with a clear way to opt out, is the standard. Take advice if in doubt.

**Two checks before the first sales call** (each takes a day, and an informed client will ask):

1. Confirm the Meydan Free Zone licence activities (licence 2540153.01) cover project management / owner's representation services delivered to onshore Dubai and Abu Dhabi clients.
2. Put professional indemnity insurance in place, or have a quote ready, and have a one-page appointment letter and terms ready to sign for each fixed-fee product.

## 1. Ideal client profiles

The best first client has a decision coming up in the next 60 days, money already committed, and a reason to want someone who has sat on the builder's side. Ranked by how fast each is likely to say yes:

### Profile A: the owner whose build has stalled or gone sour (fastest yes)

- **Who:** owner of a private villa under construction in Dubai or Abu Dhabi where progress has stopped, the contractor is claiming extensions of time and variations, payments are disputed, or the relationship with the consultant has broken down. Often the owner lives abroad or travels heavily.
- **Trigger:** a large interim payment application, a variation claim, a notice of delay, a contractor threatening to stop work, or a lawyer telling them they need an independent view of the facts.
- **Why us:** we spent 21 years building the claims they are now receiving. We know how a variation account and an extension-of-time claim are put together, and where they are weak.
- **Entry product:** stalled project rescue review (section 3).
- **How to reach them:** almost entirely through referral. UAE construction lawyers, architects and interior designers who have been brought in mid-project, and brokers who are asked to sell an unfinished villa.

- **Who to target first:** Wadeem plot owners. More than 1,700 plots sold out within 72 hours of launch in July 2025 (Modon). Many of those owners are now 12–18 months into choosing a consultant and getting a design approved, which is exactly when contract and bid review is needed. Second: Palm Jebel Ali plot owners, a smaller, wealthier, more international group.
- **Entry products:** plot / pre-design review for owners who have not appointed a consultant yet. Contract and bid review for owners who have a design and are about to choose a contractor.
- **How to reach them:** the architects and consultants they are appointing, the brokers who sold the plots, and their private bankers and wealth advisers.

### Profile C: British and European owners renovating existing prime villas

- **Where:** Palm Jumeirah, Emirates Hills, Jumeirah Islands, Al Barari, District One, Dubai Hills Estate.
- **Who:** British or European owner, often in their 40s–60s, sometimes still running a business in the UK or Europe, planning a full renovation or extension after buying a resale villa. Nakheel communities need a Nakheel NOC before the authority permit, which adds a step most first-time renovators do not expect.
- **Why us:** they recognise the UK builder background immediately (JCT contracts, a RIBA South West Award, a home on Grand Designs). They are often nervous about managing a Dubai fit-out contractor from abroad.
- **Entry products:** second-opinion budget review, or contract and bid review on the fit-out contractor's quotation.
- **How to reach them:** interior designers and fit-out architects, brokers who sold them the villa (renovation is a common conversation after a resale purchase), UK-facing wealth managers and relocation firms, and the British Chamber of Commerce Dubai.

### Profile D: small developers and investors building villas to sell

- **Who:** an individual or small company building one to five villas on plots for resale (for example the developer behind a high-profile Palm Jumeirah spec villa, or small investors on Palm Jebel Ali and Jumeirah Bay).
- **Why us:** they care about programme, cost and the quality of the final product, and they understand the value of a builder's eye on the contractor's commercial position.
- **Entry products:** development advisory, or contract and bid review. Longer sales cycle, but repeat work.
- **How to reach them:** prime brokerages (who often advise on what to build), construction lawyers, and Dubai Chambers events.

### What a good first client is not

- Someone looking for a contractor or a designer. We are neither, and we say so.
- Someone who wants us to "supervise" or sign anything for the authority. That is the consultant's role.
- A project under AED 1m construction value. The fee is hard to justify at that size, and the outcome will not make a useful reference.

## 2. Where to reach them

### 2.1 Referral partners (the fastest route)

Why referral partners first: a first client with no UAE track record buys on trust, and trust is easiest borrowed from someone they have already appointed. An architect, broker or lawyer who introduces us is, in effect, giving the reference we cannot yet give ourselves.

**What we offer partners (no money changes hands):**

- **Architects and design studios:** a client-side counterpart who keeps the owner calm, makes decisions on time and does not fight the design. We protect their fee by catching contractor cost creep before the owner blames the design.
- **Brokers:** an independent person they can introduce to a plot buyer who asks "what will it cost to build here and how long?", which helps close a plot sale without the broker giving construction advice.
- **Lawyers:** an independent, factual review of a stalled project's programme and cost position, written by someone who has built these claims. (We do not act as an expert witness or give legal advice.)
- **Wealth managers and relocation firms:** a safe pair of hands for the client's largest single spend after the purchase itself.

**How to approach:** one short, personal email to the published business address or contact form, naming one specific thing about their work, offering a 20-minute coffee or call, and attaching nothing. Follow up once after five working days, then stop.

- **What it is:** the first three clients to sign any fixed-fee review get the founding-client fee shown above (about 20–25% off). This is fair and low-risk, because a builder's eye on a set of documents almost always finds something. Philip should only adopt it if he is comfortable with it.

## 5. Discovery call script, objections and qualification

### Discovery call (30 minutes)

**1. Opening (2 minutes)**
"Thank you for making the time. I'd like to spend most of this call understanding your project, and at the end I'll tell you honestly whether we're the right people to help, and if not, who might be. Is that all right?"

**2. Their situation (10 minutes)**
- Tell me about the project. Where is the plot or villa, and where are you in the process?
- Who have you appointed so far: consultant, architect, interior designer, contractor?
- What's the budget, and how was that number arrived at?
- What does the programme look like, and what's driving the date?
- Where are you based, and how often can you be on site?

**3. The pain (8 minutes)**
- What's worrying you most right now?
- Has anything already happened that surprised you: a cost, a delay, a claim?
- If nothing changes, what does this look like in six months?
- Who else is involved in the decision: spouse, family office, lawyer?

**4. Our position (5 minutes)**
"For 21 years I ran P Chapman Construction in the UK, a premium residential builder. I priced the jobs, ran the sites and built the variation accounts and delay claims. Now we sit on the other side of the table. We don't design and we don't build. We represent you, alongside your DM-registered consultant and your contractor, and we're paid only by you."
Then name the one product that fits, its fixed fee, what they will receive, and by when.

**5. Close (5 minutes)**
- "Would it help if I sent you a one-page scope and fee for the [product] today, so you can look at it with [spouse / lawyer]?"
- Agree a specific next step and date. Send the scope within three hours of the call.

### Objection handling

**"You haven't done a project in the UAE."**
"That's right, and we won't pretend otherwise. Eastbridge is new in the UAE. What we bring is 21 years as the builder: we ran a premium residential construction company in the UK, with a RIBA South West Award (Owers House, 2016), an LABC Building Excellence Award (2018 winner) and a Highly Commended in 2020, a home featured on Grand Designs and a house reported in The Times. The things that go wrong for owners, like loose contracts, provisional sums, variations and delay claims, work the same way here. On the local side, the regulations and approvals are handled by your DM-registered consultant. We work alongside them, and we're paid only by you. That's also why we've made the first engagement a small fixed-fee review: you can judge the quality of our work before committing to anything larger."

**"My consultant already supervises the contractor."**
"They should, and that's their legal role. They are responsible for the design and the technical supervision the authority requires. We do something different: we look after your money, your programme and your contract, and we report to you alone. On a good project we make the consultant's job easier, because decisions get made on time."

**"Isn't this just another layer of fees?"**
"It's a fair question. A review costs a fixed fee, agreed in writing before we start [FEE TO BE CONFIRMED – proposed AED 7,500–15,000]. In our experience, a single uncapped provisional sum or a vague exclusion in a contractor's bid can cost far more than that. If the review doesn't find anything worth acting on, you will have paid for peace of mind. And the fee credits in full against a full appointment."

**"Can you recommend a contractor?"**
"We don't take commission from anyone, so we have no reason to push a particular firm. We'll help you set out what you need, compare the bids you receive like for like, and ask the hard questions. The choice and the appointment are yours."

**"Can you get my permit / sign off the drawings?"**
"No. Your registered consultant prepares and submits the permit application, and the permit is issued by Dubai Municipality, Trakhees or the Dubai Development Authority, depending on where your plot sits, or in Abu Dhabi by the municipality through TAMM. We represent you alongside the consultant and keep the process moving."

**"My contractor and I are in dispute. Can you be our expert?"**
"We can give you and your lawyer an independent, factual review of where the project stands: programme, payments, variations. For legal advice or formal expert evidence, you need a UAE-qualified lawyer and, if required, an appointed expert. We're happy to work alongside them."

**"Let me think about it."**
"Of course. Is there something specific you're unsure about that I can answer now? Otherwise, I'll send the scope today and call you on [day] to see where you've got to."

**"The price is too high."**
"Which part of the scope matters most to you? We can narrow it, for example to two bids instead of three, but we won't cut the time we spend on what we review."

### Qualification checklist

Proceed to a proposal when most of these are true:

- [ ] Private villa, new build or major renovation, in Dubai or Abu Dhabi
- [ ] Construction value of roughly AED 1m or more (renovation) or AED 5m or more (new build)
- [ ] A decision or trigger in the next 60 days (buying, appointing, signing, a claim)
- [ ] Speaking to the decision maker, or the decision maker will join the next call
- [ ] Wants someone on their side, not a contractor or designer
- [ ] Accepts that the consultant does the regulated work and we represent the owner
- [ ] Accepts a fixed fee paid in advance (or 50% on signing for the rescue review)
- [ ] No conflict: we have no relationship with their contractor or consultant
- [ ] Documents can be shared within a week

**Do not proceed** (refer onwards politely) if they want a contractor, a designer, someone to sign for the authority, or a free opinion with no intention of appointing.
